## g10-euc-l04-w01: Rectangle ABCD has AB=6 cm and BC=5 cm. Find AC and state why BD has the same length.

Visual contract: {"family": "grade10_euclidean", "values": {"lesson": 4, "a": 3, "b": 3, "c": 4, "model": "quadrilateral", "shape": "rectangle", "width": 6, "height": 5, "radicand": 61, "answer": "AC = BD = √61 cm"}}

1. Draw the four right angles of rectangle ABCD.

2. Label AB=6 cm and BC=5 cm.

3. In right triangle ABC, AC²=6²+5²=61.

4. AC=√61 cm; the diagonals of a rectangle are equal.

5. Answer: AC = BD = √61 cm. Square the diagonal to check the two side squares.

**Answer:** AC = BD = √61 cm. **Misconception:** Use only marked geometric facts, state the theorem, and keep corresponding vertices in order.

## g10-euc-l04-w02: Rhombus ABCD has diagonals AC=8 cm and BD=8 cm. Find its area and name the diagonal angle.

Visual contract: {"family": "grade10_euclidean", "values": {"lesson": 4, "a": 4, "b": 4, "c": 4, "model": "quadrilateral", "shape": "rhombus", "diagonal_ac": 8, "diagonal_bd": 8, "answer": "area = 32 cm²; diagonals perpendicular"}}

1. Draw a rhombus and mark all four sides equal.

2. Label diagonals AC=8 cm and BD=8 cm.

3. Rhombus diagonals bisect at right angles, forming four right triangles.

4. Area=½×8×8=32 cm².

5. Answer: area = 32 cm²; diagonals perpendicular. Their lengths need not be equal.
