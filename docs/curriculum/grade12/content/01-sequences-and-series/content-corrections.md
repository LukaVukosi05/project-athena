## Lesson 3 — worked example: consecutive arithmetic terms

- Question: `x−2, 2x+1, 5x−8` are consecutive arithmetic terms. Find `x`.
- Original answer: `x=4`.
- Corrected answer: `x=6`.
- Verification: `(2x+1)−(x−2)=x+3` and `(5x−8)−(2x+1)=3x−9`. Equating the differences gives `x+3=3x−9`, hence `12=2x` and `x=6`. The resulting terms are `4, 13, 22`, with common difference `9`.

## Lesson 3 — independent practice: inconsistent consecutive terms

- Question: `x, 3x−4, 5x−6` are consecutive arithmetic terms. Find `x`.
- Original answer: `x=2`.
- Corrected answer: no value of `x` exists; the authored question is inconsistent.
- Verification: the consecutive differences are `(3x−4)−x=2x−4` and `(5x−6)−(3x−4)=2x−2`. Equating them gives `2x−4=2x−2`, or `−4=−2`, a contradiction.
