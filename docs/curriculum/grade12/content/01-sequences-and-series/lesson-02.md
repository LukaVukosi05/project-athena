# Arithmetic sequences and the nth term

## Learning objective

Derive and use T_n = a + (n−1)d.

## Prerequisite diagnostic

Check the prerequisite concepts listed in the source teaching sequence before beginning the worked examples.

## Full concept explanation

The first term is a. To reach term n from term 1, exactly n−1 equal jumps are made. Each jump is d, therefore T_n = a + (n−1)d. The n−1 is structural, not a formula trick.

## Vocabulary and notation

Use the notation and mathematical vocabulary introduced in the concept explanation exactly as written.

## Whiteboard teaching sequence

- Frame
- Board state / animation
- Teacher narration or prompt
- 1
- Place T_1=a, T_2=a+d, T_3=a+2d on a number line.
- Count the number of jumps from term 1.
- 2
- Extend to T_n=a+(n−1)d and highlight n−1.
- Term n is n−1 jumps from the first term.
- 3
- For 7, 11, 15, … mark a=7 and d=4.
- Identify values before substituting.
- 4
- Write T_n=7+4(n−1)=4n+3.
- Expand only after the structure is correct.
- 5
- Check n=1 and n=3 in the rule.
- A valid rule reproduces known terms.

## Worked examples

- Question: Find T_25 for 7, 11, 15, …
- Worked answer: T_25=7+24(4)=103.
- Question: Find the nth term of 14, 9, 4, −1, …
- Worked answer: a=14,d=−5; T_n=14−5(n−1)=19−5n.
- Misconception repair  Using nd instead of (n−1)d. Repair by physically counting jumps: term 4 is three jumps from term 1.
