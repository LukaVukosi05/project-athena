# Solving arithmetic sequence problems

## Learning objective

Determine unknown terms, positions and parameters from arithmetic information.

## Prerequisite diagnostic

Check the prerequisite concepts listed in the source teaching sequence before beginning the worked examples.

## Full concept explanation

Translate every statement into T_n = a+(n−1)d. Two known terms produce two linear equations in a and d. Solve the equations, then return to the question and check the term position is a positive integer.

## Vocabulary and notation

Use the notation and mathematical vocabulary introduced in the concept explanation exactly as written.

## Whiteboard teaching sequence

- Frame
- Board state / animation
- Teacher narration or prompt
- 1
- Show T_5=18 and T_12=46.
- Convert term facts into equations.
- 2
- Write a+4d=18 and a+11d=46.
- The coefficient of d is one less than the term number.
- 3
- Subtract equations; highlight 7d=28.
- Elimination removes a.
- 4
- Find d=4, then a=2.
- Substitute into either original equation.
- 5
- Build T_n=2+4(n−1)=4n−2 and verify both terms.
- Always reconstruct and check the sequence.

## Worked examples

- Question: Given T_5=18 and T_12=46, find T_30.
- Worked answer: d=4,a=2; T_30=118.
- Question: Three consecutive arithmetic terms are x−2, 2x+1, 5x−8. Find x.
- Worked answer: Equal differences: (2x+1)−(x−2)=(5x−8)−(2x+1); x=4.
- Misconception repair  Dividing term values by their positions to find d. Repair: d measures change per step and must come from subtraction or simultaneous equations.
