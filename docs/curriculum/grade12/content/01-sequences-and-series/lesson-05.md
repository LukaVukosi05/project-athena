# Geometric sequences and the nth term

## Learning objective

Derive and use T_n = ar^(n−1), including negative and fractional ratios.

## Prerequisite diagnostic

Check the prerequisite concepts listed in the source teaching sequence before beginning the worked examples.

## Full concept explanation

A geometric sequence multiplies by the same ratio r. From the first term to term n there are n−1 multiplications, so T_n = ar^(n−1). A negative ratio alternates signs; |r|<1 produces decreasing magnitudes.

## Vocabulary and notation

Use the notation and mathematical vocabulary introduced in the concept explanation exactly as written.

## Whiteboard teaching sequence

- Frame
- Board state / animation
- Teacher narration or prompt
- 1
- Write a, ar, ar², ar³ under positions 1–4.
- Count repeated multiplications.
- 2
- Generalise to T_n=ar^(n−1). Highlight exponent n−1.
- The exponent counts multiplications from term 1.
- 3
- For 160,80,40,… calculate r=80/160=1/2.
- Ratio is next term divided by previous term.
- 4
- Write T_n=160(1/2)^(n−1).
- Keep brackets around a fractional or negative ratio.
- 5
- Graph term number against value.
- The change is multiplicative, not linear.

## Worked examples

- Question: Find T_9 for 3, −6, 12, −24, …
- Worked answer: a=3,r=−2; T_9=3(−2)^8=768.
- Question: Find T_n for 625,125,25,…
- Worked answer: T_n=625(1/5)^(n−1).
- Misconception repair  Finding r by subtraction. Repair: geometric change is multiplication; divide consecutive non-zero terms.
