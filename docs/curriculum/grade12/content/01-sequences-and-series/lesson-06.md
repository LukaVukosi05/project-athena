# Geometric parameters and applications

## Learning objective

Solve for unknown geometric parameters and model repeated percentage change.

## Prerequisite diagnostic

Check the prerequisite concepts listed in the source teaching sequence before beginning the worked examples.

## Full concept explanation

Use T_p=ar^(p−1) and T_q=ar^(q−1). Dividing the equations removes a and gives r^(q−p)=T_q/T_p. In context, repeated percentage growth uses r=1+p/100; decay uses r=1−p/100.

## Vocabulary and notation

Use the notation and mathematical vocabulary introduced in the concept explanation exactly as written.

## Whiteboard teaching sequence

- Frame
- Board state / animation
- Teacher narration or prompt
- 1
- Show T_3=20 and T_6=160.
- Translate each fact using the nth-term formula.
- 2
- Write ar²=20 and ar⁵=160.
- Align powers with positions.
- 3
- Divide: r³=8, so r=2.
- Division cancels a cleanly.
- 4
- Find a=5 and reconstruct T_n=5·2^(n−1).
- Return to a known equation.
- 5
- Link 8% growth to multiplier 1.08.
- Percent change becomes a repeated ratio.

## Worked examples

- Question: T_3=20,T_6=160. Find a and r.
- Worked answer: r=2,a=5.
- Question: A culture starts at 500 and grows 12% per hour. Amount after 8 completed hours?
- Worked answer: 500(1.12)^8 ≈ 1 238 organisms.
- Misconception repair  Using 12 as the ratio for 12% growth. Repair: 100%+12%=112%=1.12.
