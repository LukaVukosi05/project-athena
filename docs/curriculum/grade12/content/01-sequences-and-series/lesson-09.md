# Deriving the arithmetic-series formula

## Learning objective

Understand and apply S_n=n/2[2a+(n−1)d]=n/2(a+l).

## Prerequisite diagnostic

Check the prerequisite concepts listed in the source teaching sequence before beginning the worked examples.

## Full concept explanation

A series is the sum of sequence terms. Pairing the first and last, second and second-last, produces equal pair sums a+l. The formula is derived from structure and remains valid whether n is odd or even.

## Vocabulary and notation

Use the notation and mathematical vocabulary introduced in the concept explanation exactly as written.

## Whiteboard teaching sequence

- Frame
- Board state / animation
- Teacher narration or prompt
- 1
- Write S_n=a+(a+d)+…+[a+(n−1)d].
- Show the series forwards.
- 2
- Write the same series backwards underneath.
- Align first with last.
- 3
- Add columns: each gives 2a+(n−1)d.
- Every pair has the same sum.
- 4
- There are n columns: 2S_n=n[2a+(n−1)d].
- Count paired columns.
- 5
- Divide by 2 and show alternative n/2(a+l).
- Connect formula to first and last term.

## Worked examples

- Question: Find S_40 for 7+11+15+…
- Worked answer: 20[14+39(4)]=3 400.
- Question: Find 5+8+11+…+101.
- Worked answer: n=33; S=33/2(5+101)=1 749.
- Misconception repair  Using T_n when the question asks for S_n. Repair: T is one term; S is accumulated total.
