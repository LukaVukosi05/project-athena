# Infinite geometric series and convergence

## Learning objective

Explain convergence and use S_infinity=a/(1−r) only when |r|<1.

## Prerequisite diagnostic

Check the prerequisite concepts listed in the source teaching sequence before beginning the worked examples.

## Full concept explanation

An infinite series can have a finite sum when its terms shrink rapidly enough. For |r|<1, r^n approaches 0, so the finite formula approaches a/(1−r). If |r|≥1, terms do not shrink to zero and the geometric series diverges.

Use the notation and mathematical vocabulary introduced in the concept explanation exactly as written.

## Whiteboard teaching sequence

- Frame
- Board state / animation
- Teacher narration or prompt
- 1
- Shade 1/2, then 1/4, then 1/8 of a unit strip.
- Partial sums approach one whole.
- 2
- List S_1=1/2,S_2=3/4,S_3=7/8.
- The sums approach a limit.
- 3
- Start from S_n=a(1−r^n)/(1−r).
- Use the finite formula.
- 4
- For |r|<1 highlight r^n→0.
- The remaining tail becomes arbitrarily small.
- 5
- Conclude S_infinity=a/(1−r); contrast r=1.2 and r=−1.
- State convergence before calculating.

## Worked examples

- Question: Find 12+6+3+…
- Worked answer: a=12,r=1/2; S∞=24.
- Question: Determine convergence of 5−10+20−…
- Worked answer: r=−2, |r|>1, so it diverges.
- Misconception repair  Using S∞ whenever a series has dots. Repair: dots mean continuation, not convergence; test |r|<1.
