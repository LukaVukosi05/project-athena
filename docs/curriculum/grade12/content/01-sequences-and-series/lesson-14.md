# Infinite-series applications

## Learning objective

Model limits, recurring decimals and endless repeated motion.

## Prerequisite diagnostic

Check the prerequisite concepts listed in the source teaching sequence before beginning the worked examples.

## Full concept explanation

An infinite model is justified only when the process repeats indefinitely with a constant ratio whose magnitude is below one. The mathematical limit may describe an idealised total even when the physical process eventually stops.

## Vocabulary and notation

Use the notation and mathematical vocabulary introduced in the concept explanation exactly as written.

## Whiteboard teaching sequence

- Frame
- Board state / animation
- Teacher narration or prompt
- 1
- Convert 0.272727… into 0.27+0.0027+0.000027+…
- Expose the geometric structure.
- 2
- Identify a=27/100,r=1/100.
- Each block shifts two decimal places.
- 3
- S∞=(27/100)/(99/100)=27/99=3/11.
- Simplify exactly.
- 4
- Show a bouncing ball: initial drop h and repeated up/down distances.
- Separate the first drop from the repeated geometric part.
- 5
- Total distance = h+2(rebound-height sum).
- Each rebound height is travelled upward and downward.

## Worked examples

- Question: Write 0.363636… as a fraction.
- Worked answer: (36/100)/(1−1/100)=36/99=4/11.
- Question: Ball dropped 10 m, rebound ratio 0.6. Ideal total distance?
- Worked answer: 10+2[6/(1−0.6)]=40 m.
- Misconception repair  Doubling the initial drop in bounce problems. Repair: only rebound heights are travelled twice; the initial drop occurs once.
