## Mastery objective

Move fluently among standard, factorised and turning-point forms.

Duration: 50–65 minutes

Mastery objective: Move fluently among standard, factorised and turning-point forms.

Stable lesson ID: g12-functions-04

Mastery threshold: 80% with targeted remediation

## Teach the idea

Standard form exposes the y-intercept and concavity, factorised form exposes roots, and turning-point form exposes the vertex. Equivalent forms describe the same parabola.

## Whiteboard sequence

| Frame | Board state and animation | Narration / learner action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Show y=x²−6x+5 in standard form. | Explain, predict, calculate or verify this change before continuing. |
| 2 | Factor to (x−1)(x−5) and highlight roots. | Explain, predict, calculate or verify this change before continuing. |
| 3 | Complete the square to (x−3)²−4 as a teaching transformation. | Explain, predict, calculate or verify this change before continuing. |
| 4 | Overlay all three equations on the same graph. | Explain, predict, calculate or verify this change before continuing. |
| 5 | Label vertex, axis of symmetry, intercepts and range. | Explain, predict, calculate or verify this change before continuing. |

## Worked examples

Question: Analyse y=−2(x−1)²+8.

Worked answer: Vertex (1,8), maximum 8, axis x=1, opens down.

Question: Rewrite x²+4x−5 in turning-point form.

Worked answer: (x+2)²−9.

Misconception repair  Assuming the signs inside brackets are the vertex coordinates. Repair: (x−p)²+q has vertex (p,q).
