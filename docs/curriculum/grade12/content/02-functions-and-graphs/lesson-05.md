## Mastery objective

Determine a quadratic equation from roots, a point or a turning point.

Duration: 50–65 minutes

Mastery objective: Determine a quadratic equation from roots, a point or a turning point.

Stable lesson ID: g12-functions-05

Mastery threshold: 80% with targeted remediation

## Teach the idea

Choose the form matching the given information. Roots suggest y=a(x−p)(x−q); a turning point suggests y=a(x−p)²+q. Use the additional point to solve the scale factor a.

## Whiteboard sequence

| Frame | Board state and animation | Narration / learner action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Display roots (−2,0),(4,0) and point (0,8). | Explain, predict, calculate or verify this change before continuing. |
| 2 | Write y=a(x+2)(x−4). | Explain, predict, calculate or verify this change before continuing. |
| 3 | Substitute (0,8) and solve a=−1. | Explain, predict, calculate or verify this change before continuing. |
| 4 | Write y=−(x+2)(x−4) and calculate vertex. | Explain, predict, calculate or verify this change before continuing. |
| 5 | Verify all three supplied points graphically. | Explain, predict, calculate or verify this change before continuing. |

## Worked examples

Question: Roots 1 and 5; point (0,10).

Worked answer: y=2(x−1)(x−5).

Question: Vertex (2,−3); point (4,5).

Worked answer: y=2(x−2)²−3.

Misconception repair  Setting a=1 when the shape merely looks standard. Repair: the extra point determines vertical stretch and reflection.
