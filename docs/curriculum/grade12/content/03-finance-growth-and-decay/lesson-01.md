## Mastery objective

Distinguish simple growth, compound growth and decay, and identify every variable with units.

Duration: 50–65 minutes

Stable lesson ID: g12-finance-01

Mastery objective: Distinguish simple growth, compound growth and decay, and identify every variable with units.

Mastery threshold: 80% with targeted remediation

## Teach the idea

Financial mathematics models how money changes over time. Start with a cash-flow timeline: mark date 0, the valuation date, every payment, the compounding frequency and the direction in which value is being moved. Simple interest acts on the original principal; compound interest acts on the changing balance.

Formula and notation anchor  Simple growth: A=P(1+in). Compound growth: A=P(1+i)^n.

## Whiteboard sequence

| Frame | Board state and animation | Narration / learner action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Orient: place P at date 0 and A at the valuation date. | Orient and identify the focal date. |
| 2 | Inspect the wording for simple/compound, annual rate and term. | Predict the required operation. |
| 3 | Convert the percentage to a decimal and attach units to i and n. | Calculate with visible units. |
| 4 | Animate one simple-interest step beside one compound-interest step. | Explain why the state is equivalent. |
| 5 | Verify that growth gives A>P and state the interest earned. | Verify and summarise. |

## Worked examples

Question: R8 000 earns 9% simple interest for 30 months. Find A.

Worked method: n=2.5 years. A=8 000(1+0.09×2.5)=R9 800.

Question: R8 000 earns 9% p.a. compounded annually for 2.5 years. Find A.

Worked method: A=8 000(1.09)^2.5≈R9 923.30; the model permits a fractional annual period only when the rate convention supports it.

Misconception repair  Using the same formula whenever the word ‘interest’ appears. Repair by identifying what balance earns interest in each period.

## Progressive support

| Support stage | Learner-facing action |
| --- | --- |
| Hint 1 — strategy | Draw or inspect the timeline. Name the focal date and decide whether values move forward or backward. |
| Hint 2 — structure | Write the periodic rate i and period/payment count n with units before selecting the formula. |
| Hint 3 — checkpoint | Substitute without rounding, calculate, then test direction, magnitude, timing and units against the context. |

## Worked-solution presentation

- Reveal the model and timeline before the numerical result.

- Give every algebraic line a plain-language ‘Why?’ explanation.

- Highlight the first invalid step when repairing an attempt.

- End with a financial interpretation and an independent verification.

Exit checkpoint  The learner must explain—in words—why the chosen focal date, periodic rate and period count belong together. Correct calculation without this explanation is supported success, not yet independent mastery.
