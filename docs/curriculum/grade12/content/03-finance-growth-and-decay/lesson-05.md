## Mastery objective

Move a present amount forward to a specified valuation date.

Duration: 50–65 minutes

Stable lesson ID: g12-finance-05

Mastery objective: Move a present amount forward to a specified valuation date.

Mastery threshold: 80% with targeted remediation

## Teach the idea

Future value accumulates money from an earlier date to a later date. The exponent is the number of compounding intervals crossed, not simply the number printed next to the word ‘years’.

Formula and notation anchor  F=P(1+i)^n.

## Whiteboard sequence

| Frame | Board state and animation | Narration / learner action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Mark P and the future valuation date on a timeline. | Orient and identify the focal date. |
| 2 | Count compounding intervals with visible tick marks. | Predict the required operation. |
| 3 | Attach one growth factor to each interval. | Calculate with visible units. |
| 4 | Substitute P, periodic i and n. | Explain why the state is equivalent. |
| 5 | Verify using repeated multiplication for a short version. | Verify and summarise. |

## Worked examples

Question: R25 000 is invested for 6 years at 8.75% p.a. compounded quarterly.

Worked method: F=25 000(1+0.0875/4)^24≈R42 022.96.

Question: What principal grows to R50 000 in 8 years at 9% annually?

Worked method: P=50 000/(1.09)^8≈R25 093.31.

Misconception repair  Counting six periods because the investment lasts six years despite quarterly compounding. Repair by drawing all four periods per year.

## Progressive support

| Support stage | Learner-facing action |
| --- | --- |
| Hint 1 — strategy | Draw or inspect the timeline. Name the focal date and decide whether values move forward or backward. |
| Hint 2 — structure | Write the periodic rate i and period/payment count n with units before selecting the formula. |
| Hint 3 — checkpoint | Substitute without rounding, calculate, then test direction, magnitude, timing and units against the context. |

## Worked-solution presentation

- Reveal the model and timeline before the numerical result.

- Give every algebraic line a plain-language ‘Why?’ explanation.

- Highlight the first invalid step when repairing an attempt.

- End with a financial interpretation and an independent verification.

Exit checkpoint  The learner must explain—in words—why the chosen focal date, periodic rate and period count belong together. Correct calculation without this explanation is supported success, not yet independent mastery.
