## Mastery objective

Discount a future amount back to its equivalent present value.

Duration: 50–65 minutes

Stable lesson ID: g12-finance-06

Mastery objective: Discount a future amount back to its equivalent present value.

Mastery threshold: 80% with targeted remediation

## Teach the idea

Present value answers how much must exist now to produce a stated future amount. Discounting reverses accumulation, so divide by the growth factor or use a negative exponent.

Formula and notation anchor  P=F(1+i)^−n = F/(1+i)^n.

## Whiteboard sequence

| Frame | Board state and animation | Narration / learner action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Place F at the future date and P at date 0. | Orient and identify the focal date. |
| 2 | Draw the valuation arrow backward in time. | Predict the required operation. |
| 3 | Count the intervals crossed. | Calculate with visible units. |
| 4 | Animate division by (1+i) for each interval. | Explain why the state is equivalent. |
| 5 | Re-accumulate P to verify the stated F. | Verify and summarise. |

## Worked examples

Question: How much is needed now for R180 000 in 4 years at 9% p.a. compounded monthly?

Worked method: P=180 000(1+0.09/12)^−48≈R125 750.54.

Question: A debt of R60 000 is due in 3 years. Find its value now at 8% compounded quarterly.

Worked method: P=60 000(1.02)^−12≈R47 309.59.

Misconception repair  Swapping P and F. Repair by labelling the actual dates before selecting the unknown.

## Progressive support

| Support stage | Learner-facing action |
| --- | --- |
| Hint 1 — strategy | Draw or inspect the timeline. Name the focal date and decide whether values move forward or backward. |
| Hint 2 — structure | Write the periodic rate i and period/payment count n with units before selecting the formula. |
| Hint 3 — checkpoint | Substitute without rounding, calculate, then test direction, magnitude, timing and units against the context. |

## Worked-solution presentation

- Reveal the model and timeline before the numerical result.

- Give every algebraic line a plain-language ‘Why?’ explanation.

- Highlight the first invalid step when repairing an attempt.

- End with a financial interpretation and an independent verification.

Exit checkpoint  The learner must explain—in words—why the chosen focal date, periodic rate and period count belong together. Correct calculation without this explanation is supported success, not yet independent mastery.
