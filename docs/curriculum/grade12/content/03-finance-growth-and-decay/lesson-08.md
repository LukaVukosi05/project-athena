## Mastery objective

Calculate the accumulated value of regular deposits immediately after the final payment.

Duration: 50–65 minutes

Stable lesson ID: g12-finance-08

Mastery objective: Calculate the accumulated value of regular deposits immediately after the final payment.

Mastery threshold: 80% with targeted remediation

## Teach the idea

A future-value annuity moves every equal deposit to a common later valuation date. The last payment earns no interest when valuation occurs immediately after it; the first payment earns n−1 periods.

Formula and notation anchor  S=x[((1+i)^n−1)/i].

## Whiteboard sequence

| Frame | Board state and animation | Narration / learner action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Draw n equal deposits and the valuation point after payment n. | Orient and identify the focal date. |
| 2 | Move the first deposit across n−1 periods. | Predict the required operation. |
| 3 | Move the last deposit across zero periods. | Calculate with visible units. |
| 4 | Group the geometric series and reveal the formula. | Explain why the state is equivalent. |
| 5 | Verify with a three-payment direct expansion. | Verify and summarise. |

## Worked examples

Question: Deposit R1 500 monthly for 5 years at 9.6% p.a. compounded monthly.

Worked method: S=1 500[((1+0.096/12)^60−1)/(0.096/12)]≈R114 935.80.

Question: Find the deposit needed for R200 000 after 4 years at 8% monthly.

Worked method: x=200 000(0.08/12)/[(1+0.08/12)^48−1]≈R3 549.25.

Misconception repair  Giving the final payment one period of interest. Repair by anchoring the valuation immediately after that payment.

## Progressive support

| Support stage | Learner-facing action |
| --- | --- |
| Hint 1 — strategy | Draw or inspect the timeline. Name the focal date and decide whether values move forward or backward. |
| Hint 2 — structure | Write the periodic rate i and period/payment count n with units before selecting the formula. |
| Hint 3 — checkpoint | Substitute without rounding, calculate, then test direction, magnitude, timing and units against the context. |

## Worked-solution presentation

- Reveal the model and timeline before the numerical result.

- Give every algebraic line a plain-language ‘Why?’ explanation.

- Highlight the first invalid step when repairing an attempt.

- End with a financial interpretation and an independent verification.

Exit checkpoint  The learner must explain—in words—why the chosen focal date, periodic rate and period count belong together. Correct calculation without this explanation is supported success, not yet independent mastery.
