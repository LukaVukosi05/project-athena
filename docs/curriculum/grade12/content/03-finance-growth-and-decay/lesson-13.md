## Mastery objective

Use logarithms to solve growth, decay and annuity equations when the exponent is unknown.

Duration: 50–65 minutes

Stable lesson ID: g12-finance-13

Mastery objective: Use logarithms to solve growth, decay and annuity equations when the exponent is unknown.

Mastery threshold: 80% with targeted remediation

## Teach the idea

When the unknown occurs in an exponent, first isolate the exponential factor, then take logarithms. The answer may need to be interpreted as a whole number of compounding or payment periods depending on the context.

Formula and notation anchor  If A=P(1+i)^n, then n=log(A/P)/log(1+i).

## Whiteboard sequence

| Frame | Board state and animation | Narration / learner action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Identify the unknown exponent and isolate its power. | Orient and identify the focal date. |
| 2 | Take log of both sides. | Predict the required operation. |
| 3 | Bring the exponent down using log laws. | Calculate with visible units. |
| 4 | Solve n and convert periods to years if needed. | Explain why the state is equivalent. |
| 5 | Interpret whether the next whole period is required. | Verify and summarise. |

## Worked examples

Question: How long does money take to double at 8% compounded annually?

Worked method: n=log2/log1.08≈9.006 years; it first exceeds double after 10 complete annual compounding periods.

Question: An asset falls from R300 000 to R120 000 at 15% reducing balance. Find n.

Worked method: n=log(120/300)/log(0.85)≈5.64 years as an algebraic fractional-period result; apply the question’s contextual period rule.

Misconception repair  Rounding n down when the target must be reached or exceeded. Repair by testing the adjacent whole periods against the original inequality.

| Hint 2 — structure | Write the periodic rate i and period/payment count n with units before selecting the formula. |
| Hint 3 — checkpoint | Substitute without rounding, calculate, then test direction, magnitude, timing and units against the context. |

## Worked-solution presentation

- Reveal the model and timeline before the numerical result.

- Give every algebraic line a plain-language ‘Why?’ explanation.

- Highlight the first invalid step when repairing an attempt.

- End with a financial interpretation and an independent verification.

Exit checkpoint  The learner must explain—in words—why the chosen focal date, periodic rate and period count belong together. Correct calculation without this explanation is supported success, not yet independent mastery.
