## Mastery objective

Model cash flows when rates, deposits, withdrawals or payment conditions change.

Duration: 50–65 minutes

Stable lesson ID: g12-finance-14

Mastery objective: Model cash flows when rates, deposits, withdrawals or payment conditions change.

Mastery threshold: 80% with targeted remediation

## Teach the idea

A change in rate or cash flow splits the problem into phases. Choose one valuation date, move every amount to that date using the correct rate for each interval, and never apply a later rate to earlier periods.

Formula and notation anchor  Value at focal date = sum of each cash flow moved to that date through its own phase factors.

## Whiteboard sequence

| Frame | Board state and animation | Narration / learner action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Draw the entire timeline before writing a formula. | Orient and identify the focal date. |
| 2 | Mark the exact rate-change date with a vertical divider. | Predict the required operation. |
| 3 | Place deposits and withdrawals with directional arrows. | Calculate with visible units. |
| 4 | Move every cash flow to one focal date phase by phase. | Explain why the state is equivalent. |
| 5 | Verify each exponent by counting intervals on the diagram. | Verify and summarise. |

## Worked examples

Question: R20 000 grows 3 years at 7% then 2 years at 9%, annually.

Worked method: A=20 000(1.07)^3(1.09)^2≈R29 109.47.

Question: R50 000 is invested; R8 000 is withdrawn after 2 years; value one year later at 10% annually.

Worked method: A=[50 000(1.10)^2−8 000](1.10)=R57 750.

Misconception repair  Applying the second rate to the entire term. Repair by splitting the timeline at the change and attaching a factor only to intervals it spans.

| --- | --- |
| Hint 1 — strategy | Draw or inspect the timeline. Name the focal date and decide whether values move forward or backward. |
| Hint 2 — structure | Write the periodic rate i and period/payment count n with units before selecting the formula. |
| Hint 3 — checkpoint | Substitute without rounding, calculate, then test direction, magnitude, timing and units against the context. |

## Worked-solution presentation

- Reveal the model and timeline before the numerical result.

- Give every algebraic line a plain-language ‘Why?’ explanation.

- Highlight the first invalid step when repairing an attempt.

- End with a financial interpretation and an independent verification.

Exit checkpoint  The learner must explain—in words—why the chosen focal date, periodic rate and period count belong together. Correct calculation without this explanation is supported success, not yet independent mastery.
