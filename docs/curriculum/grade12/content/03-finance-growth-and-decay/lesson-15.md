## Mastery objective

Compare loans or investments fairly using equivalent rates, cash-flow timing and total financial effect.

Duration: 50–65 minutes

Stable lesson ID: g12-finance-15

Mastery objective: Compare loans or investments fairly using equivalent rates, cash-flow timing and total financial effect.

Mastery threshold: 80% with targeted remediation

## Teach the idea

A fair comparison uses the same valuation date and includes deposits, fees, instalments, balloon payments and compounding conventions. A lower nominal rate is not necessarily the cheaper or better option.

Formula and notation anchor  Compare effective rate, present value, future value or total cost at one clearly stated date.

## Whiteboard sequence

| Frame | Board state and animation | Narration / learner action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Place both options on aligned timelines. | Orient and identify the focal date. |
| 2 | Convert rates to comparable effective rates. | Predict the required operation. |
| 3 | Add every fee and special payment at its true date. | Calculate with visible units. |
| 4 | Value both options at the same focal date. | Explain why the state is equivalent. |
| 5 | State the decision with a quantitative reason and a limitation. | Verify and summarise. |

## Worked examples

Question: Option A: 10% nominal monthly; Option B: 10.1% effective. Which has lower annual growth?

Worked method: A effective=(1+0.10/12)^12−1≈10.47%, so B at 10.1% is lower.

Question: Why is ‘smallest instalment’ not enough to choose a loan?

Worked method: Terms, fees, deposit, balloon payment and total repayment period may differ.

Misconception repair  Comparing quoted percentages without matching compounding conventions. Repair by comparing effective annual rates or values at one date.

| Question | Answer / expected conclusion |
| --- | --- |
| 10% nominal monthly effective | 10.47% |

| Common comparison date | A stated focal date |

## Progressive support

| Support stage | Learner-facing action |
| --- | --- |
| Hint 1 — strategy | Draw or inspect the timeline. Name the focal date and decide whether values move forward or backward. |
| Hint 2 — structure | Write the periodic rate i and period/payment count n with units before selecting the formula. |
| Hint 3 — checkpoint | Substitute without rounding, calculate, then test direction, magnitude, timing and units against the context. |

## Worked-solution presentation

- Reveal the model and timeline before the numerical result.

- Give every algebraic line a plain-language ‘Why?’ explanation.

- Highlight the first invalid step when repairing an attempt.

- End with a financial interpretation and an independent verification.

Exit checkpoint  The learner must explain—in words—why the chosen focal date, periodic rate and period count belong together. Correct calculation without this explanation is supported success, not yet independent mastery.
