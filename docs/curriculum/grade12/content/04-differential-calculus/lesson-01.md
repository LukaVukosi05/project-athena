## Mastery objective

Explain rate of change as output change per unit input change and interpret its units.

Duration: 50-70 minutes

Stable lesson ID: g12-calculus-01

Mastery objective: Explain rate of change as output change per unit input change and interpret its units.

Mastery threshold: 80% with conceptual explanation required

## Teach the idea before the formula

A rate does not describe an output alone. It compares how much the output changes with how much the input changes. The units are therefore output-units per input-unit.

Formula anchor  Average rate = change in output / change in input = Delta y / Delta x.

Where it comes from and why it works  The formula is a ratio because a change of 20 metres means something different over 2 seconds and over 10 seconds. Division normalises the output change to one input unit.

| Frame | Board state and animation | Narration / learner action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Mark the start and end inputs. | Orient: name the target quantity and what is known. |
| 2 | Calculate both outputs before subtracting. | Inspect: connect symbols to graph objects or changing quantities. |
| 3 | Show Delta y and Delta x as separate directed changes. | Operate: show the cause before the changed expression. |
| 4 | Divide using the same subtraction order. | Explain: state why the transition is mathematically valid. |
| 5 | Interpret the sign and compound units in context. | Verify: check direction, units, coordinates, signs or boundaries. |

## Worked examples

Question: A tank volume changes from 22 L at 2 min to 55 L at 5 min.

Worked reasoning: (55-22)/(5-2)=11 L/min. The volume increased by an average of 11 litres each minute.

Question: Temperature falls from 18 C to 6 C in 4 h.

Worked reasoning: (6-18)/4=-3 C/h. The negative sign means a decrease.

Misconception repair  Calling 33 litres the rate. Repair by asking: over how many minutes did that change occur?

- Begin with a one-sentence target statement: 'I need to find ...'.

- Reveal the formula's visual meaning before using its compressed symbolic form.

- Give each algebraic line a plain-language 'Why?' explanation.

- Highlight the first invalid step when repairing an attempt rather than only showing the final memo.

- Finish with an independent verification: graph behaviour, substitution, sign analysis, units, domain or boundary comparison.
