## Mastery objective

Calculate average gradient from a function and connect the algebra to two points on its graph.

Duration: 50-70 minutes

Mastery threshold: 80% with conceptual explanation required

## Teach the idea before the formula

The average gradient of a curve over an interval is the gradient of the straight secant joining the interval endpoints. Function notation supplies the two y-values.

Formula anchor  m_avg = [f(b)-f(a)]/(b-a).

Where it comes from and why it works  The straight-line gradient formula is applied to the points (a,f(a)) and (b,f(b)). Nothing new is memorised: the function only generates the coordinates.

## Worked examples

Question: For f(x)=x^2-3x, find the average gradient from x=1 to x=4.

Worked reasoning: f(1)=-2, f(4)=4; m=(4-(-2))/(4-1)=2.

Question: For g(x)=2x^2+5 from x=-1 to x=3.

Worked reasoning: g(-1)=7, g(3)=23; m=(23-7)/4=4.

Misconception repair  Using b-a on the bottom but f(a)-f(b) on top. Repair by reading both changes in the same direction.
