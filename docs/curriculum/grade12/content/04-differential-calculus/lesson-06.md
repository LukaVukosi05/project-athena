## Teach the idea before the formula

First principles turns the secant-to-tangent story into an exact algebraic process. Each part of the formula has a geometric job rather than being a string to memorise.

Formula anchor  f'(x) = limit as h approaches 0 of [f(x+h)-f(x)]/h.

Where it comes from and why it works  f(x+h)-f(x) is the vertical change, h is the horizontal change, and the limit changes an average gradient into an instantaneous gradient.

## Whiteboard reasoning sequence

| Frame | Board state and animation | Narration / learner action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Colour-code f(x+h), f(x), the subtraction and h. | Orient: name the target quantity and what is known. |
| 2 | Point each symbol to its graph feature. | Inspect: connect symbols to graph objects or changing quantities. |
| 3 | Substitute the function without skipping brackets. | Operate: show the cause before the changed expression. |
| 4 | Simplify while h remains non-zero. | Explain: state why the transition is mathematically valid. |
| 5 | Apply the limit and interpret the resulting function. | Verify: check direction, units, coordinates, signs or boundaries. |

## Worked examples

Question: Explain the numerator.

Worked reasoning: It is the change in y between two nearby curve points.

Question: Explain the denominator.

Worked reasoning: It is the horizontal separation from x to x+h, equal to h.

Misconception repair  Memorising the order but not the meaning, then writing f(x)-f(x+h). Repair by tracing the change from the fixed point to the nearby point.

## Detailed worked-solution presentation

- Begin with a one-sentence target statement: 'I need to find ...'.

- Reveal the formula's visual meaning before using its compressed symbolic form.

- Give each algebraic line a plain-language 'Why?' explanation.

- Highlight the first invalid step when repairing an attempt rather than only showing the final memo.

- Finish with an independent verification: graph behaviour, substitution, sign analysis, units, domain or boundary comparison.

Explain-back exit checkpoint  The learner must explain why the formula or derivative condition applies here. A correct answer with no explanation is recorded as supported procedural success, not yet independent conceptual mastery.
