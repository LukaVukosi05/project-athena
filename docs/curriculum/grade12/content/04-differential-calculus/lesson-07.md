Mastery threshold: 80% with conceptual explanation required

## Teach the idea before the formula

Notation tells us whether we want a derivative function, a derivative value at a point, or a rate with named variables. The symbols differ, but they all represent instantaneous change.

Formula anchor  f'(x), y', dy/dx and D_x[f(x)] denote differentiation; f'(a) is the gradient at x=a.

Where it comes from and why it works  The prime notation emphasises a new function. Leibniz notation emphasises output change relative to input change. Substituting a number into the derivative gives a single gradient.

## Whiteboard reasoning sequence

| Frame | Board state and animation | Narration / learner action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Display one curve and its derivative function. | Orient: name the target quantity and what is known. |
| 2 | Match each notation to the same slope field. | Inspect: connect symbols to graph objects or changing quantities. |
| 3 | Separate f'(x) from f'(2). | Operate: show the cause before the changed expression. |
| 4 | Translate dy/dx into 'rate of y with respect to x'. | Explain: state why the transition is mathematically valid. |
| 5 | Check units after evaluating at a point. | Verify: check direction, units, coordinates, signs or boundaries. |

## Worked examples

Question: If f'(x)=3x^2-4, find f'(2).

Worked reasoning: f'(2)=12-4=8.

Question: For s(t), what does ds/dt mean?

Worked reasoning: Instantaneous change of displacement with respect to time: velocity.

Misconception repair  Treating f'(2) as a coordinate rather than a gradient value. Repair by asking what output the derivative function produces.

## Detailed worked-solution presentation

- Begin with a one-sentence target statement: 'I need to find ...'.

- Reveal the formula's visual meaning before using its compressed symbolic form.

- Give each algebraic line a plain-language 'Why?' explanation.

- Highlight the first invalid step when repairing an attempt rather than only showing the final memo.

- Finish with an independent verification: graph behaviour, substitution, sign analysis, units, domain or boundary comparison.

Explain-back exit checkpoint  The learner must explain why the formula or derivative condition applies here. A correct answer with no explanation is recorded as supported procedural success, not yet independent conceptual mastery.
