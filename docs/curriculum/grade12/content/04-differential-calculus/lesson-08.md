## Mastery objective

Differentiate a polynomial from first principles and justify every algebraic transition.

Duration: 50-70 minutes

Stable lesson ID: g12-calculus-08

Mastery objective: Differentiate a polynomial from first principles and justify every algebraic transition.

Mastery threshold: 80% with conceptual explanation required

## Teach the idea before the formula

The method is substitution, subtraction, expansion, factorisation, cancellation and then a limit. The cancellation is legal because the secant stage uses h not equal to zero.

Formula anchor  For f(x)=x^2: f'(x)=lim [((x+h)^2-x^2)/h]=lim(2x+h)=2x.

Where it comes from and why it works  Expanding reveals a common factor h. Cancelling that factor removes the removable obstruction; the simplified secant-gradient expression can then approach its tangent value.

## Whiteboard reasoning sequence

| Frame | Board state and animation | Narration / learner action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Write f(x+h) with full brackets. | Orient: name the target quantity and what is known. |
| 2 | Subtract the complete f(x). | Inspect: connect symbols to graph objects or changing quantities. |
| 3 | Expand and cancel like terms. | Operate: show the cause before the changed expression. |
| 4 | Factor h before cancelling it. | Explain: state why the transition is mathematically valid. |
| 5 | Apply the limit only after simplification. | Verify: check direction, units, coordinates, signs or boundaries. |

## Worked examples

Question: Differentiate f(x)=x^2+3x from first principles.

Worked reasoning: The quotient simplifies to 2x+h+3; the limit gives f'(x)=2x+3.

Question: Differentiate f(x)=3x^2 from first principles.

Worked reasoning: The quotient simplifies to 6x+3h; hence f'(x)=6x.

Misconception repair  Cancelling h across addition before factorising. Repair by requiring h to be a factor of the entire numerator first.

## Detailed worked-solution presentation

- Begin with a one-sentence target statement: 'I need to find ...'.

- Reveal the formula's visual meaning before using its compressed symbolic form.

- Give each algebraic line a plain-language 'Why?' explanation.

- Highlight the first invalid step when repairing an attempt rather than only showing the final memo.

- Finish with an independent verification: graph behaviour, substitution, sign analysis, units, domain or boundary comparison.

Explain-back exit checkpoint  The learner must explain why the formula or derivative condition applies here. A correct answer with no explanation is recorded as supported procedural success, not yet independent conceptual mastery.
