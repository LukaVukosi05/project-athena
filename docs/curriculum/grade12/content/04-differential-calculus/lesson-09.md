## Teach the idea before the formula

Differentiation asks how a power changes locally. The exponent becomes a multiplier because expansion of (x+h)^n creates n identical first-order x^(n-1)h contributions; higher powers of h vanish in the limit.

Formula anchor  d/dx(ax^n) = an x^(n-1).

Where it comes from and why it works  The rule is a compressed result of first principles. 'Bring down and subtract one' is the visible pattern, but its reason is the coefficient of the h-term in the binomial expansion.

## Worked examples

Question: Differentiate 5x^4.

Worked reasoning: 5 multiplied by 4 gives 20, and 4-1 gives 3: 20x^3.

Question: Differentiate -3x^-2.

Worked reasoning: (-3)(-2)x^-3=6x^-3.

Misconception repair  Changing the coefficient but forgetting to reduce the exponent. Repair by tracking the two separate jobs of n.
