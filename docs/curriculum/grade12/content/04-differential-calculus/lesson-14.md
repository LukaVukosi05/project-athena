## Mastery objective

Find stationary points by solving f'(x)=0 and recover their coordinates.

Duration: 50-70 minutes

Mastery threshold: 80% with conceptual explanation required

## Teach the idea before the formula

A stationary point occurs where the tangent is horizontal. Horizontal lines have gradient zero, so stationary x-values solve f'(x)=0.

Formula anchor  Stationary condition: f'(x)=0; coordinates are (x,f(x)).

Where it comes from and why it works  The equation f'(x)=0 finds where local change pauses. It does not give the y-coordinate, which must come from the original function.

## Worked examples

Question: f(x)=x^3-3x^2+2.

Worked reasoning: f'=3x(x-2), so x=0,2. Points: (0,2) and (2,-2).

Question: f(x)=x^2-4x+1.

Worked reasoning: f'=2x-4=0 gives x=2; point (2,-3).

Misconception repair  Reporting only x-values. Repair by remembering that a point requires both x and y.
