## Mastery objective

Classify stationary points as local maxima or minima using sign changes or the second derivative.

Duration: 50-70 minutes

Stable lesson ID: g12-calculus-15

Mastery objective: Classify stationary points as local maxima or minima using sign changes or the second derivative.

Mastery threshold: 80% with conceptual explanation required

## Teach the idea before the formula

A local maximum changes from increasing to decreasing; a local minimum changes from decreasing to increasing. The derivative's sign makes that behaviour visible.

Formula anchor  At x=a: f' changes + to - gives local max; - to + gives local min.

Where it comes from and why it works  The derivative is positive where the graph rises and negative where it falls. Classification follows from the direction change on the two sides, not from the y-value being globally largest or smallest.

## Worked examples

Question: For f=x^3-3x, classify x=-1 and x=1.

Worked reasoning: f'=3(x-1)(x+1): +,-,+; x=-1 is local max and x=1 local min.

Question: Why is 'local' important?

Worked reasoning: It compares nearby values, not necessarily every value in the domain.

Misconception repair  Calling the larger stationary y-value an absolute maximum without considering the unbounded cubic. Repair by using local direction language.

## Detailed worked-solution presentation

- Begin with a one-sentence target statement: 'I need to find ...'.

- Reveal the formula's visual meaning before using its compressed symbolic form.

- Give each algebraic line a plain-language 'Why?' explanation.

- Highlight the first invalid step when repairing an attempt rather than only showing the final memo.

- Finish with an independent verification: graph behaviour, substitution, sign analysis, units, domain or boundary comparison.

Explain-back exit checkpoint  The learner must explain why the formula or derivative condition applies here. A correct answer with no explanation is recorded as supported procedural success, not yet independent conceptual mastery.
