## Mastery objective

Interpret f''(x) as the rate at which the gradient changes and use it as a classification tool.

Duration: 50-70 minutes

Stable lesson ID: g12-calculus-16

Mastery objective: Interpret f''(x) as the rate at which the gradient changes and use it as a classification tool.

Mastery threshold: 80% with conceptual explanation required

## Teach the idea before the formula

The first derivative describes slope. Differentiating it again describes how that slope changes. Positive f'' means slopes are increasing; negative f'' means slopes are decreasing.

Formula anchor  f''(x)=d/dx[f'(x)]. At stationary x=a: f''(a)>0 suggests min; f''(a)<0 suggests max.

Where it comes from and why it works  Near a stationary point, increasing slopes pass from negative through zero to positive, producing a minimum. Decreasing slopes pass from positive through zero to negative, producing a maximum.

## Worked examples

Question: f=x^3-3x at x=-1 and 1.

Worked reasoning: f''=6x; f''(-1)<0 max, f''(1)>0 min.

Question: What if f''(a)=0?

Worked reasoning: The test is inconclusive; inspect f' signs or further behaviour.

Misconception repair  Assuming f''=0 proves a point of inflection. Repair by requiring a concavity change.
