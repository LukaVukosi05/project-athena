## Mastery objective

Find and verify points where concavity changes.

Duration: 50-70 minutes

Stable lesson ID: g12-calculus-18

Mastery objective: Find and verify points where concavity changes.

Mastery threshold: 80% with conceptual explanation required

## Teach the idea before the formula

A point of inflection is where the graph changes concavity. Solving f''(x)=0 gives candidates, but the sign of f'' must change across the candidate.

Formula anchor  Candidate condition: f''(x)=0; proof condition: f'' changes sign.

Where it comes from and why it works  Zero second derivative only says the gradient's rate of change is zero at that instant. Inflection requires different concavity on the two sides.

## Whiteboard reasoning sequence

| Frame | Board state and animation | Narration / learner action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Solve f''(x)=0 for candidate x-values. | Orient: name the target quantity and what is known. |
| 2 | Create intervals around each candidate. | Inspect: connect symbols to graph objects or changing quantities. |
| 3 | Test the sign of f'' on both sides. | Operate: show the cause before the changed expression. |
| 4 | Substitute verified x into f for y. | Explain: state why the transition is mathematically valid. |
| 5 | Mark the concavity change on the curve. | Verify: check direction, units, coordinates, signs or boundaries. |

## Worked examples

Question: f=x^3-3x^2+2.

Worked reasoning: f''=6x-6=0 at x=1; sign changes - to +; f(1)=0, so (1,0).

Question: Why does f=x^4 at x=0 not have an inflection?

Worked reasoning: f''=12x^2 is non-negative on both sides, so concavity does not change.

Misconception repair  Stopping after f''=0. Repair by making the sign-change row compulsory.

## Detailed worked-solution presentation

- Begin with a one-sentence target statement: 'I need to find ...'.

- Reveal the formula's visual meaning before using its compressed symbolic form.

- Give each algebraic line a plain-language 'Why?' explanation.

- Highlight the first invalid step when repairing an attempt rather than only showing the final memo.

- Finish with an independent verification: graph behaviour, substitution, sign analysis, units, domain or boundary comparison.

Explain-back exit checkpoint  The learner must explain why the formula or derivative condition applies here. A correct answer with no explanation is recorded as supported procedural success, not yet independent conceptual mastery.
