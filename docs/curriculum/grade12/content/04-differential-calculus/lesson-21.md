## Mastery objective

Interpret derivatives as instantaneous rates in contextual non-motion problems.

Duration: 50-70 minutes

Stable lesson ID: g12-calculus-21

Mastery objective: Interpret derivatives as instantaneous rates in contextual non-motion problems.

Mastery threshold: 80% with conceptual explanation required

## Teach the idea before the formula

If a quantity Q depends on time, Q'(t) describes how quickly it changes at each instant. Its units are Q-units per time-unit, and its sign states increase or decrease.

Formula anchor  Instantaneous rate at t=a is Q'(a).

Where it comes from and why it works  The derivative function is formed before the time is substituted because the function describes the rate process; evaluating at a selects the requested instant.

## Worked examples

Question: V(t)=t^3-6t^2+20t litres. Find rate at t=3.

Worked reasoning: V'=3t^2-12t+20; V'(3)=11 L/min, so volume is increasing at 11 L/min.

Question: P(t)=500-8t^2 people. Rate at t=4.

Worked reasoning: P'=-16t; P'(4)=-64 people/hour, a decrease.

Misconception repair  Reporting Q(a) instead of Q'(a). Repair by naming one as amount and the other as rate.

## Detailed worked-solution presentation

- Begin with a one-sentence target statement: 'I need to find ...'.

- Reveal the formula's visual meaning before using its compressed symbolic form.

- Give each algebraic line a plain-language 'Why?' explanation.

- Highlight the first invalid step when repairing an attempt rather than only showing the final memo.

- Finish with an independent verification: graph behaviour, substitution, sign analysis, units, domain or boundary comparison.

Explain-back exit checkpoint  The learner must explain why the formula or derivative condition applies here. A correct answer with no explanation is recorded as supported procedural success, not yet independent conceptual mastery.
