## Mastery objective

Connect displacement, velocity and acceleration using first and second derivatives.

Duration: 50-70 minutes

Stable lesson ID: g12-calculus-22

Mastery objective: Connect displacement, velocity and acceleration using first and second derivatives.

Mastery threshold: 80% with conceptual explanation required

## Teach the idea before the formula

Displacement gives position relative to an origin. Velocity is the rate of displacement change; acceleration is the rate of velocity change. Signs describe direction, while speed is the non-negative magnitude of velocity.

Formula anchor  v(t)=s'(t); a(t)=v'(t)=s''(t); speed=|v(t)|.

Where it comes from and why it works  Each derivative moves one level down the motion chain because it measures how the previous quantity changes per unit time.

## Whiteboard reasoning sequence

| Frame | Board state and animation | Narration / learner action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Align s(t), v(t) and a(t) in three rows. | Orient: name the target quantity and what is known. |
| 2 | Differentiate s to obtain v. | Inspect: connect symbols to graph objects or changing quantities. |
| 3 | Differentiate v to obtain a. | Operate: show the cause before the changed expression. |
| 4 | Solve v=0 for rest or direction-change candidates. | Explain: state why the transition is mathematically valid. |
| 5 | Use signs and units to interpret motion. | Verify: check direction, units, coordinates, signs or boundaries. |

## Worked examples

Question: s=t^3-6t^2+9t. Find v and a.

Worked reasoning: v=3t^2-12t+9; a=6t-12.

Question: When is the particle at rest?

Worked reasoning: v=3(t-1)(t-3)=0, so t=1 s and t=3 s.

Misconception repair  Calling negative velocity negative speed. Repair by separating direction (velocity sign) from magnitude (absolute value).

## Detailed worked-solution presentation

- Begin with a one-sentence target statement: 'I need to find ...'.

- Reveal the formula's visual meaning before using its compressed symbolic form.

- Give each algebraic line a plain-language 'Why?' explanation.

- Highlight the first invalid step when repairing an attempt rather than only showing the final memo.

- Finish with an independent verification: graph behaviour, substitution, sign analysis, units, domain or boundary comparison.

Explain-back exit checkpoint  The learner must explain why the formula or derivative condition applies here. A correct answer with no explanation is recorded as supported procedural success, not yet independent conceptual mastery.
