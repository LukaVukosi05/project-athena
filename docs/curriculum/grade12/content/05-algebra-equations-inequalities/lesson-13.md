## Teach the idea before the procedure

Nature-of-roots questions are condition problems. Solve the discriminant condition for the parameter, not for x.

| STRUCTURE ANCHOR Set Delta >0, =0 or <0 and retain the condition a!=0. |
| --- |

## Whiteboard reasoning sequence

| Frame | Board and narration requirement |
| --- | --- |
| 1 - Orient | Display the full task. Name what must be found and the conditions already visible. |
| 2 - Inspect | Highlight structure: standard form, factors, radical, denominator, repeated power, parameter or interval. |
| 3 - Select | State the chosen method and why it matches this structure. Ask the learner to predict the first valid operation. |
| 4 - Operate | Animate each equivalence-preserving step; keep the previous line faintly visible and explain its validity. |
| 5 - Verify | Substitute, test intervals, check restrictions or inspect the discriminant. Reject invalid candidates explicitly. |
| 6 - Summarise | State the answer, the method, the misconception to avoid and the transfer rule for the next problem. |

## Support and completion

| Stage | Learner-facing action |
| --- | --- |
| Hint 1 - strategy | Name the target and identify the structure without performing the calculation. |
| Hint 2 - first step | Show the first valid transformation and its reason; do not reveal the final answer. |
| Hint 3 - checkpoint | Expose the critical value, restriction, substitution or discriminant calculation that unlocks the method. |
| Worked solution | Reveal numbered steps with an expression, explanation, why, focus target and verification. |
| Exit explain-back | Learner explains why the chosen method applies and how the answer was checked. |

| LESSON PASS At least 10 of 12 practice items correct, including both exam-level items, plus a valid explain-back. Any unresolved PARAMETER/ROOT-CONDITION error routes to targeted repair and a fresh parallel item. |
