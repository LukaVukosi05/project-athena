## Teach the idea before the procedure

A proof explains why a statement holds for every allowed value; checking one example is evidence, not proof.

| STRUCTURE ANCHOR Define a general integer or non-negative square, transform logically, conclude. |
| --- |

## Whiteboard reasoning sequence

| Frame | Board and narration requirement |
| --- | --- |
| 1 - Orient | Display the full task. Name what must be found and the conditions already visible. |
| 2 - Inspect | Highlight structure: standard form, factors, radical, denominator, repeated power, parameter or interval. |
| 3 - Select | State the chosen method and why it matches this structure. Ask the learner to predict the first valid operation. |
| 4 - Operate | Animate each equivalence-preserving step; keep the previous line faintly visible and explain its validity. |
| 5 - Verify | Substitute, test intervals, check restrictions or inspect the discriminant. Reject invalid candidates explicitly. |
| 6 - Summarise | State the answer, the method, the misconception to avoid and the transfer rule for the next problem. |

## Ten whiteboard examples

| ID | Level | Prompt | Expected reasoning / conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G12-ALG-L15-W01 | Foundation | Show that n(n+1) is even for integer n. | Consecutive integers: one is even, so the product is even. |
| G12-ALG-L15-W02 | Foundation | Prove (a+b)^2-(a-b)^2=4ab. | Expand both squares and simplify. |
| G12-ALG-L15-W03 | Routine | Show that the square of an odd integer is odd. | Let n=2k+1; n^2=2(2k^2+2k)+1. |
| G12-ALG-L15-W04 | Routine | Prove x^2+y^2>=2xy. | (x-y)^2>=0 implies x^2-2xy+y^2>=0. |
| G12-ALG-L15-W05 | Routine | Show that n^2+n+2 is even for integer n. | n(n+1) is even and adding 2 preserves evenness. |
| G12-ALG-L15-W06 | Complex | Explain why 2^m cannot equal a negative number for real m. | A positive base raised to a real power is positive. |
| G12-ALG-L15-W07 | Complex | Prove the product of two consecutive even integers is divisible by 8. | Let them be 2k and 2(k+1); product=4k(k+1), and k(k+1) is even. |
| G12-ALG-L15-W08 | Complex | Show (x+1)(x-1)=x^2-1 geometrically or algebraically. | Distributive law gives x^2-x+x-1. |
| G12-ALG-L15-W09 | Exam-level | Prove if x+y is odd, one of x,y is even and the other odd. | Same parity sums are even; therefore parities differ. |
| G12-ALG-L15-W10 | Exam-level | Explain why squaring an equation can introduce an extraneous root. | Squaring removes sign information; A^2=B^2 allows A=±B. |

Whiteboard rule: reveal one operation at a time. Each example must include a learner prediction, a validity explanation and an independent verification.

## Twelve self-guided questions

| ID | Level | Question | Answer and memo route |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G12-ALG-L15-P01 | Foundation | Show that x^2+4x+5 is always positive. | Complete square: (x+2)^2+1>0. |
| G12-ALG-L15-P02 | Foundation | Prove the sum of three consecutive integers is divisible by 3. | (n-1)+n+(n+1)=3n. |
| G12-ALG-L15-P03 | Routine | Show that if a quadratic has roots r and s then x^2-(r+s)x+rs=0. | Expand (x-r)(x-s)=0. |
| G12-ALG-L15-P04 | Routine | Explain why division by an expression requires a restriction. | The divisor cannot equal zero. |
| G12-ALG-L15-P05 | Routine | Show x+1/x>=2 for x>0. | (x-1)^2>=0; divide by positive x. |
| G12-ALG-L15-P06 | Routine | Prove an integer divisible by 6 is divisible by both 2 and 3. | n=6k=2(3k)=3(2k). |
| G12-ALG-L15-P07 | Complex | Show the difference of consecutive squares is odd. | (n+1)^2-n^2=2n+1. |
| G12-ALG-L15-P08 | Complex | Explain why a zero discriminant gives one repeated root. | The ±sqrt(Delta) part becomes zero. |
| G12-ALG-L15-P09 | Complex | Prove (x-y)^2=(y-x)^2. | y-x=-(x-y); squaring removes the sign. |
| G12-ALG-L15-P10 | Complex | Show x^2-x is even for integer x. | x(x-1) is a product of consecutive integers. |
| G12-ALG-L15-P11 | Exam-level | Prove no real x satisfies x^2+1=0. | x^2>=0, so x^2+1>=1. |
| G12-ALG-L15-P12 | Exam-level | Explain consistent accuracy in a multi-step solution. | A later correct method may earn method marks even after an earlier arithmetic error. |

## Support and completion

| Stage | Learner-facing action |
| --- | --- |
| Hint 1 - strategy | Name the target and identify the structure without performing the calculation. |
| Hint 2 - first step | Show the first valid transformation and its reason; do not reveal the final answer. |
| Hint 3 - checkpoint | Expose the critical value, restriction, substitution or discriminant calculation that unlocks the method. |
| Worked solution | Reveal numbered steps with an expression, explanation, why, focus target and verification. |
| Exit explain-back | Learner explains why the chosen method applies and how the answer was checked. |

| LESSON PASS At least 10 of 12 practice items correct, including both exam-level items, plus a valid explain-back. Any unresolved PROOF/JUSTIFICATION error routes to targeted repair and a fresh parallel item. |
