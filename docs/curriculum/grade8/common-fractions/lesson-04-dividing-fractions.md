# Grade 8 Common Fractions — Lesson 4: Dividing Fractions

## Teaching design

Show the dividend as equal units and mark the size of one divisor group. Count complete and partial groups before naming the quotient. Change both fractions into the same-sized pieces and compare counts; show the reciprocal calculation as a check, never as an unexplained shortcut.

Each example has a five-stage board: identify operands, establish equal units, perform the visible operation, simplify, and verify. The answer is hidden in the initial state.

### g8-frac-l04-w01
Question: How many halves are in 3 wholes?
Visual: measured grouping strip of 3 and 1/2; labels and selected portions use these values.
1. Ask how many groups of size 1/2 fit in 3; draw the original amount and the group size.
2. Partition both amounts into 2ths: 3 is 6/2 and 1/2 is 1/2.
3. Count groups: (6/2) ÷ (1/2) = 6/1 = 6. A remainder can be part of a group.
4. Answer: 6.
5. 3 ÷ 1/2 = 6. Check that 6 × 1/2 = 3.
Misconception repair: Divide equal-sized parts; a partial group is a fraction, not an ignored remainder. Zero cannot be a divisor.
