# Grade 8 Exponents · Lesson 2: Laws of exponents

Scope: 2026 DBE Grade 8 Mathematics ATP Term 2, Exponents. All questions and explanations below are original. Teacher review pending.

## Objective
Derive and apply the same-base product and quotient laws, the power of a product and the zero-exponent law; avoid negative exponent outcomes.

## Why it matters
Powers compress repeated multiplication. Showing the equal factors makes exponent laws explainable rather than rules to memorise.

## Intuition and prerequisites
The exponent counts factors. Negative bases need brackets; scientific notation preserves the whole number by compensating each decimal shift. Before Lesson 2, understand expanded powers and equal factor cancellation.

## Whiteboard playback

## Worked example bank

### Worked example 1 · Foundation
**Question:** Evaluate 2² × 2³ using an exponent law.
**Answer:** 32
**Full memo:**
1. Identify the common base 2; the two powers have exponents 2 and 3.
2. Expand 2² into 2 copies of 2.
3. Expand 2³ into another 3 copies of 2.
4. Together there are 2+3=5 equal factors.
5. Apply the product law: 2² × 2³ = 2⁵.
6. Evaluate 2⁵ = 32.
**Check:** 4 × 8 = 32; multiplying the original powers agrees.
**Repair:** Same base: add exponents, never bases.

### Worked example 3 · Easy
**Question:** Use an exponent law to evaluate 8⁰.
**Answer:** 1
**Full memo:**
1. Start with a nonzero base 8.
2. Write 8² ÷ 8²; equal nonzero quantities divide to 1.
3. Apply the quotient law to the same expression: 8^(2−2) = 8⁰.
4. The exponent difference is 0, so 8⁰ = 1.
5. Do not mistake the zero exponent for multiplication by zero.
**Check:** 8² ÷ 8² = 1; the original base is nonzero.
**Repair:** A nonzero base to exponent zero equals one.

### Worked example 5 · Standard
**Question:** Use a power-of-a-product law to evaluate 6² by factoring 6 as 2 × 3.
**Answer:** 36
**Full memo:**
1. Factor the original base 6 as 2 × 3.
2. Rewrite the original power: 6² = (2 × 3)².
3. Expand into 2 copies of (2 × 3); this is multiplication, not addition.
4. Regroup equal factors to give 2² × 3².
5. Evaluate both smaller powers: 2² = 4 and 3² = 9.
6. Multiply 4 × 9 = 36.
**Check:** 6² = 36; raising the original whole base gives the same result.
**Repair:** Only products allow this law: (3+2)² = 25, whereas 3²+2² = 13. Factor the original base as a multiplication first.

### Worked example 9 · Intermediate
**Question:** Simplify and evaluate 3⁴ × 3² ÷ 3³. Show each exponent law.
**Answer:** 27
**Full memo:**
1. All three powers share nonzero base 3; the exponents are 4, 2 and 3.
2. Combine the numerator factors first: 3⁴ × 3² = 3⁶.
3. Divide by 3³; cancel 3 matching factors from numerator and denominator.
4. Subtract only now: (4+2)−3 = 3.
5. The expression becomes 3³.
6. Evaluate 3³ = 27.
**Check:** (81 × 9) ÷ 27 = 27; the original expression agrees.
**Repair:** Combine equal bases in the numerator, then cancel nonzero factors before subtracting exponents.
