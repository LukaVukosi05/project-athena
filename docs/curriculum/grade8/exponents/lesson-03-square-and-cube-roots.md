# Grade 8 Exponents · Lesson 3: Square and cube roots

Scope: DBE 2026 Grade 8 Mathematics ATP. Original questions; teacher review pending.

Objective: Find and verify exact square and cube roots; interpret the root in geometry.

The visual starts with the whole question and hides the answer. Each numbered line is one narrated mathematical action. The square grid, cube layers or expression lanes are introduced as the relevant calculation occurs. Previously completed lines remain visible. Learners independently submit before seeing a memo.

## Worked example 1 · foundation

**Question:** Find √36. Show why your answer is the principal square root.

**Whiteboard model:** root_square_cube; use 36 as question-specific parameters.

**Timeline:**

1. Read √36 as the nonnegative number whose square is 36.
2. Build a square with 36 equal unit tiles.
3. Arrange the tiles into 6 rows of 6 tiles.
4. The side count is 6; 6 × 6 = 36.
5. Therefore √36 = 6; the √ sign asks for the nonnegative root.

**Answer after teaching:** 6
**Verify:** 6² = 36; this reconstructs the original number.
**First-error repair:** The principal √ is nonnegative; check by squaring the proposed side.

## Worked example 2 · easy

**Question:** Find ∛64. Show the three equal dimensions.

**Whiteboard model:** root_square_cube; use 64 as question-specific parameters.

**Timeline:**

1. Read ∛64 as the number that gives 64 when cubed.
2. Build a cube from 64 equal unit cubes.
3. Count 4 unit cubes along each of the three edges.
4. Check 4 × 4 × 4 = 64.
5. Therefore ∛64 = 4.

**Answer after teaching:** 4
**Verify:** 4³ = 64; this restores the original cube.
**First-error repair:** Cube root asks for three equal factors, not division by three.

## Worked example 5 · standard

**Question:** Evaluate √81 + ∛125. Show the two roots separately.

**Whiteboard model:** root_square_cube; use 81, 125 as question-specific parameters.

**Timeline:**

1. Keep √81 and ∛125 separate: their indices describe different shapes.
2. Arrange 81 tiles into a 9 by 9 square, so √81 = 9.
3. Arrange 125 cubes into a 5 by 5 by 5 cube, so ∛125 = 5.
4. Substitute the two root values: 9 + 5.
5. Add to obtain 14.

**Answer after teaching:** 14
**Verify:** 9² = 81 and 5³ = 125; 9 + 5 = 14.
**First-error repair:** Evaluate each root with its own index before adding.

## Worked example 9 · intermediate

**Question:** A square has area 169 cm². Find its side length and perimeter.

**Whiteboard model:** root_square_cube; use 169 as question-specific parameters.

**Timeline:**

1. Label the equal sides s. Area = s × s = 169 cm².
2. Take the nonnegative square root: s = √169.
3. Arrange 169 square centimetres in a 13 by 13 square.
4. So the side length is 13 cm.
5. The perimeter is four sides: 4 × 13 = 52 cm.

**Answer after teaching:** 13 cm; 52 cm
**Verify:** 13 × 13 = 169 cm², and four sides total 52 cm.
**First-error repair:** Area has square units, perimeter has linear units; a square has four equal sides.

## Worked example 10 · exam

**Question:** A cube has volume 343 cm³. Find one edge length.

**Whiteboard model:** root_square_cube; use 343 as question-specific parameters.

**Timeline:**

2. Therefore s³ = 343 cm³.
3. Take the cube root: s = ∛343.
4. Build 7 equal layers, each 7 by 7 unit cubes.
5. The edge is 7 cm.

**Answer after teaching:** 7 cm
**Verify:** 7 × 7 × 7 = 343 cm³; the volume is restored.
**First-error repair:** Volume counts three equal dimensions; an edge uses linear units.
