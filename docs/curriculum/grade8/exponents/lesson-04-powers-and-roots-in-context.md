# Grade 8 Exponents · Lesson 4: Powers and roots in context

Scope: DBE 2026 Grade 8 Mathematics ATP. Original questions; teacher review pending.

Objective: Solve multi-step problems using powers and roots with correct order and units.

The visual starts with the whole question and hides the answer. Each numbered line is one narrated mathematical action. The square grid, cube layers or expression lanes are introduced as the relevant calculation occurs. Previously completed lines remain visible. Learners independently submit before seeing a memo.

## Worked example 1 · foundation

**Question:** Evaluate 2³ + √36 × ∛8. Show the order of operations.

**Whiteboard model:** root_application; use 2, 3, 36, 8 as question-specific parameters.

**Timeline:**

1. Identify the power 2³ and the two roots before multiplying or adding.
2. Evaluate the power: 2³ = 8.
3. Evaluate the square root: √36 = 6 because 6² = 36.
4. Evaluate the cube root: ∛8 = 2 because 2³ = 8.
5. Multiply before adding: 6 × 2 = 12.
6. Add the two terms: 8 + 12 = 20.

**Answer after teaching:** 20
**Verify:** 8 + 6 × 2 = 20; both roots recover 36 and 8.
**First-error repair:** Work through powers and roots, then multiplication, then addition.

## Worked example 2 · easy

**Question:** A square has area 64 cm². Find its side length and perimeter.

**Whiteboard model:** root_application; use 64 as question-specific parameters.

**Timeline:**

1. Label the equal sides s. Area = s × s = 64 cm².
2. Take the nonnegative square root: s = √64.
3. Arrange 64 square centimetres in a 8 by 8 square.
4. So the side length is 8 cm.
5. The perimeter is four sides: 4 × 8 = 32 cm.

**Answer after teaching:** 8 cm; 32 cm
**Verify:** 8 × 8 = 64 cm², and four sides total 32 cm.
**First-error repair:** Area has square units, perimeter has linear units; a square has four equal sides.
