# Grade 8 Exponents · Lesson 6: Integrated powers and roots

Scope: 2026 DBE Grade 8 Mathematics ATP. All questions are original; teacher review pending.

**Objective:** Solve connected power, root and measurement questions with correct order and units.

Each example begins with a complete question. The answer and memo are gated. The whiteboard shows a question-specific claim/operation trace or measured model; it reveals one line and its mathematical object at a time, retaining previous lines. Independent practice and assessment use unseen values.

## Worked example 1 · foundation

**Question:** Two square picture frames have areas 36 cm² and 64 cm². How many centimetres longer is the larger perimeter?

**Whiteboard:** exponent_context_measure with values 36, 64.

**Narrated mathematical actions:**

1. Each area is the side multiplied by itself: s₁²=36, s₂²=64.
2. Find the first side: √36=6 cm; a 6 by 6 square has area 36.
3. Find the second side: √64=8 cm; a 8 by 8 square has area 64.
4. The first perimeter is 4 × 6 = 24 cm.
5. The second perimeter is 4 × 8 = 32 cm.
6. Subtract perimeters: 32 − 24 = 8 cm.

**Answer after teaching:** 8 cm
**Verification:** 6²=36, 8²=64, and 32−24=8 cm.
**Misconception repair:** Find both sides from their areas, then compare linear perimeters, not areas.

## Worked example 2 · easy

**Question:** Two cubic boxes have volumes 27 cm³ and 125 cm³. How much longer is one edge of the larger box?

**Whiteboard:** exponent_context_measure with values 27, 125.

**Narrated mathematical actions:**

1. A cube has three equal edges; V=s³.
2. Take ∛27=3 cm, because 3 × 3 × 3 = 27.
3. Take ∛125=5 cm, because 5 × 5 × 5 = 125.
4. Compare edge lengths rather than subtracting volumes.
5. Subtract 5 − 3 = 2 cm.

**Answer after teaching:** 2 cm
**Verification:** 3³=27, 5³=125; the edge difference is 2 cm.
**Misconception repair:** Find both cube roots before comparing edge lengths; keep cubic and linear units separate.

## Worked example 3 · easy

**Question:** One square display has side 2³ cm; another has area 100 cm². Find the difference between their areas.

**Whiteboard:** exponent_context_measure with values 2, 3, 100.

**Narrated mathematical actions:**

1. First evaluate the given side: 2³=8 cm.
2. Square that side to get the first area: 8 × 8=64 cm².
3. The other display already has area 100 cm².
4. Its side would be √100=10 cm; check 10²=100.
5. Subtract the smaller area from the larger: 100−64=36 cm².

**Answer after teaching:** 36 cm²
**Verification:** 2³=8; 8²=64, and |64−100|=36 cm².
**Misconception repair:** A side written as a power must be evaluated, then squared to obtain its area.

## Worked example 4 · standard

**Question:** Compare 2⁴ with √81 × ∛8. By how much do the two values differ?

**Whiteboard:** exponent_context_measure with values 2, 4, 81, 8.

**Narrated mathematical actions:**

1. Keep the power on the left and product of roots on the right.
2. Evaluate the left: 2⁴ = 16.
3. Evaluate √81 = 9; 9² = 81.
4. Evaluate ∛8 = 2; 2³ = 8.
5. Multiply the right: 9 × 2 = 18.
6. Compare 16 and 18; difference = 18 − 16 = 2.

**Answer after teaching:** 2
**Verification:** 16 and 9 × 2 = 18; the absolute difference is 2.
**Misconception repair:** Evaluate each power and root before comparing the two complete sides.

## Worked example 5 · standard

**Question:** Two square garden beds have areas 36 m² and 64 m². A cubic planter has volume 27 m³. Find the sum of both bed perimeters and one planter edge, in metres.

**Whiteboard:** exponent_context_measure with values 36, 64, 27.

**Narrated mathematical actions:**

1. Label the first bed side √36 and the second √64.
2. Find √36 = 6 m, since 6² = 36.
3. Find √64 = 8 m, since 8² = 64.
4. Find the planter edge ∛27 = 3 m, since 3³ = 27.
5. The bed perimeters are 4 × 6 = 24 m and 4 × 8 = 32 m.
6. Add lengths only: 24 + 32 + 3 = 59 m.

**Answer after teaching:** 59 m
**Verification:** 6²=36, 8²=64, 3³=27; 24+32+3=59 m.
**Misconception repair:** Convert two areas and one volume into lengths before adding; never add unlike units.
