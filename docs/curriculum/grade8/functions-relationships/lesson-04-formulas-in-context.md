# Grade 8 Functions and Relationships — Lesson 4: Formulas in context and reverse calculations

Original Athena draft mapped to DBE Grade 8 CAPS §2.2 and Term 4 clarification. Teacher review required before release.

CAPS: https://www.education.gov.za/LinkClick.aspx?fileticket=uCNqOwfGbmc%3D&tabid=

Every board shows the actual rectangle values and hides calculated sides until the teaching step. Previous values remain on the board. The operation appears before the result; final dimensions are checked against the given area or perimeter.

## Worked examples

### 01 — foundation (2 marks)

**Question:** A rectangle is 4 cm long and 3 cm wide. Use A = l × w to find its area.
**Answer:** 12 cm²
**Scene:** {'length': 4, 'width': 3, 'area': 12, 'units': 'cm'}
**Whiteboard sequence:** A = l × w → A = 4 × w → A = 4 × 3 → A = 12 cm²
**Teaching actions:** Identify the given length and width. → Substitute both values into the formula. → Multiply four by three and attach square-centimetre units.
**Verification:** 4 cm × 3 cm = 12 cm², matching the rectangle.
**Misconception:** Adding sides instead of multiplying for area.
**Repair:** Cover a 4-by-3 grid with twelve unit squares.

### 02 — easy (2 marks)

**Question:** A rectangle is 7 cm long and 2 cm wide. Find its area using A = l × w.
**Answer:** 14 cm²
**Scene:** {'length': 7, 'width': 2, 'area': 14, 'units': 'cm'}
**Whiteboard sequence:** A = l × w → A = 7 × w → A = 7 × 2 → A = 14 cm²
**Teaching actions:** Locate the seven-centimetre length. → Replace width with two centimetres. → Count fourteen square centimetres in seven rows of two.
**Verification:** 7 × 2 = 14 cm².
**Misconception:** Reporting 14 cm instead of square centimetres.
**Repair:** Count the squares; area has square units.

### 03 — easy (3 marks)

**Question:** A rectangle has area 24 cm² and width 4 cm. Find its length.
**Answer:** 6 cm
**Scene:** {'length': 6, 'width': 4, 'area': 24, 'units': 'cm'}
**Whiteboard sequence:** A = l × w → 24 = l × 4 → l = 24 ÷ 4 → l = 6 cm
**Teaching actions:** Write the area formula before choosing a calculation. → Replace the known area and width. → Undo multiplication by four: twenty-four divided by four is six.
**Verification:** 6 cm × 4 cm = 24 cm².
**Misconception:** Multiplying 24 by four to recover a side.
**Repair:** The unknown length multiplied by width gives the area; divide to undo.

### 04 — standard (3 marks)

**Question:** A rectangle has area 35 cm² and length 7 cm. Find its width.
**Answer:** 5 cm
**Scene:** {'length': 7, 'width': 5, 'area': 35, 'units': 'cm'}
**Whiteboard sequence:** A = l × w → 35 = 7 × w → w = 35 ÷ 7 → w = 5 cm
**Teaching actions:** Start with the same rectangle area formula. → Put the known thirty-five and seven into their places. → Divide the area by seven to isolate the width.
**Verification:** 7 cm × 5 cm = 35 cm².
**Misconception:** Confusing the given length with the unknown width.
**Repair:** Label the formula slots first, then calculate.

### 05 — standard (3 marks)

**Question:** A rectangle has area 54 m² and width 6 m. Find its length.
**Answer:** 9 m
**Scene:** {'length': 9, 'width': 6, 'area': 54, 'units': 'm'}
**Whiteboard sequence:** A = l × w → 54 = l × 6 → l = 54 ÷ 6 → l = 9 m
**Teaching actions:** Identify that metres squared describe area. → Substitute the given area and width. → Divide by six metres to recover nine metres of length.
**Verification:** 9 m × 6 m = 54 m².
**Misconception:** Keeping m² as the unit of a one-dimensional side.
**Repair:** Area divided by a length leaves metres.

### 06 — standard (4 marks)

**Question:** A rectangular field is 8 m wide and 12 m long. Give its area and perimeter.
**Answer:** Area 96 m²; perimeter 40 m
**Scene:** {'length': 12, 'width': 8, 'area': 96, 'perimeter': 40, 'units': 'm'}
**Whiteboard sequence:** A = l × w; P = 2(l + w) → A = 12 × 8 = 96 m² → P = 2(12 + 8) → P = 40 m
**Teaching actions:** Use the interior grid for area and the boundary for perimeter. → Multiply twelve by eight for square metres. → Add length and width, then double for the outside boundary.
**Verification:** Area is 96 m² and boundary length is 40 m.
**Misconception:** Using one formula for both area and perimeter.
**Repair:** Shade the interior separately from tracing the four sides.

### 07 — intermediate (4 marks)

**Question:** A rectangular garden has area 48 m² and width 6 m. Find its length and perimeter.
**Answer:** Length 8 m; perimeter 28 m
**Scene:** {'length': 8, 'width': 6, 'area': 48, 'perimeter': 28, 'units': 'm'}
**Whiteboard sequence:** A = l × w; P = 2(l + w) → 48 = l × 6, so l = 8 m → P = 2(8 + 6) → P = 28 m
**Teaching actions:** First recover the missing dimension from the area. → Forty-eight divided by six gives eight metres. → Only now use both side lengths in the perimeter formula.
**Verification:** 8 × 6 = 48 m²; 2(8 + 6) = 28 m.
**Misconception:** Using area in place of length inside the perimeter formula.
**Repair:** Label sides on the rectangle before tracing its boundary.

### 08 — intermediate (4 marks)

**Question:** A rectangle has area 7.5 m² and width 2.5 m. Find its length.
**Answer:** 3 m
**Scene:** {'length': 3, 'width': 2.5, 'area': 7.5, 'units': 'm'}
**Whiteboard sequence:** A = l × w → 7.5 = l × 2.5 → l = 7.5 ÷ 2.5 → l = 3 m
**Teaching actions:** Keep decimals in their correct formula places. → The unknown length times two and a half is seven and a half. → Divide seven and a half by two and a half to get three.
**Verification:** 3 × 2.5 = 7.5 m².
**Misconception:** Rounding 2.5 before the inverse calculation.
**Repair:** Estimate first: three groups of 2.5 give 7.5 exactly.

### 09 — intermediate (4 marks)

**Question:** A rectangle is (x + 2) cm long and 3 cm wide. Write its area in two equivalent forms and find it when x = 4.
**Answer:** 3(x + 2) = 3x + 6; area 18 cm²
**Scene:** {'length': 6, 'width': 3, 'area': 18, 'units': 'cm', 'symbolic_length': 'x+2'}
**Whiteboard sequence:** A = l × w → A = 3(x + 2) → A = 3x + 6 → A(4) = 18 cm²
**Teaching actions:** The entire length is x plus two. → Multiply the entire bracket by the width three. → Distribute three, then substitute four into either form.
**Verification:** 3(4 + 2) = 18 and 3×4 + 6 = 18.
**Misconception:** Multiplying only x and forgetting the two.
**Repair:** Show three rows of x-and-two tiles before distributing.

### 10 — exam (5 marks)

**Question:** A floor has area 60 m². Its width is 5 m. Tiles cost R80 per square metre. Find the length and total tile cost.
**Answer:** Length 12 m; cost R4800
**Scene:** {'length': 12, 'width': 5, 'area': 60, 'units': 'm'}
**Whiteboard sequence:** 60 = l × 5 → l = 60 ÷ 5 = 12 m → cost = 60 × 80 → cost = R4800
**Teaching actions:** Use area and width to find the missing side. → Divide sixty by five and check the area. → Tile price is per square metre, so multiply by area, not by perimeter.
**Verification:** 12 × 5 = 60 m²; 60 × R80 = R4800.
**Misconception:** Charging by boundary length instead of floor area.
**Repair:** Highlight the square-metre unit in the rate and in the area.

### 11 — exam (5 marks)

**Question:** A rectangle has perimeter 30 cm and length 9 cm. Find its width and area.
**Answer:** Width 6 cm; area 54 cm²
**Scene:** {'length': 9, 'width': 6, 'area': 54, 'perimeter': 30, 'units': 'cm'}
**Whiteboard sequence:** P = 2(l + w) → 30 = 2(9 + w) → 15 = 9 + w, so w = 6 cm → A = 9 × 6 = 54 cm²
**Teaching actions:** Half the perimeter to obtain one length plus one width. → Fifteen equals nine plus the unknown width. → Subtract nine, then multiply length and width for area.
**Verification:** 2(9 + 6) = 30 cm; 9 × 6 = 54 cm².
**Misconception:** Treating 30 as one length plus one width.
**Repair:** Trace all four sides, then group equal sides in pairs.

### 12 — exam (5 marks)

**Question:** Two rectangles both have area 36 cm². A is 9 cm long; B is 6 cm wide. Find the missing sides and compare their perimeters.
**Answer:** A width 4 cm; B length 6 cm; perimeters 26 cm and 24 cm
**Scene:** {'length': 9, 'width': 4, 'area': 36, 'perimeter': 26, 'units': 'cm', 'comparison': {'length': 6, 'width': 6, 'area': 36, 'perimeter': 24}}
**Whiteboard sequence:** A width = 36 ÷ 9 → A width = 4 cm; B length = 36 ÷ 6 = 6 cm → P(A) = 2(9 + 4) = 26 cm → P(B) = 2(6 + 6) = 24 cm
**Teaching actions:** Each rectangle has its own known dimension. → Divide area by its matching side separately for A and B. → Use each pair of side lengths to compare boundaries.
**Verification:** Both products are 36 cm² but their perimeters differ by 2 cm.
**Misconception:** Assuming equal areas mean equal perimeters.
**Repair:** Draw both rectangles with actual side labels, then trace boundaries.

### 13 — advanced (6 marks)

**Question:** A rectangle has area 72 cm² and its length is twice its width. Find both dimensions.
**Answer:** Width 6 cm; length 12 cm
**Scene:** {'length': 12, 'width': 6, 'area': 72, 'units': 'cm'}
**Whiteboard sequence:** A = l × w; l = 2w → 72 = (2w) × w → w² = 36 → w = 6 cm; l = 12 cm
**Teaching actions:** Replace the length with twice the width in the area formula. → Two times width squared is seventy-two. → Width is positive, so its square of thirty-six gives six.
**Verification:** 12 × 6 = 72 cm² and 12 is twice 6.
**Misconception:** Accepting a negative dimension or dividing 72 by two only.
**Repair:** Sketch two equal width segments along the longer side.

### 14 — advanced (6 marks)

**Question:** A rectangular board has area 84 cm² and length (x + 5) cm, width x cm. If x = 7, verify the area and give the perimeter.
**Answer:** Area 84 cm²; perimeter 38 cm
**Scene:** {'length': 12, 'width': 7, 'area': 84, 'perimeter': 38, 'units': 'cm'}
**Whiteboard sequence:** l = x + 5, w = x → l = 12 cm, w = 7 cm → A = 12 × 7 = 84 cm² → P = 2(12 + 7) = 38 cm
**Teaching actions:** Use seven to determine the two separate side lengths. → The longer side is seven plus five, or twelve. → Multiply for area, then add both sides and double for perimeter.
**Verification:** 84 agrees with the stated area and four sides total 38 cm.
**Misconception:** Using x + 5 as the perimeter without counting all sides.
**Repair:** Trace two lengths and two widths after verifying area.

### 15 — advanced (7 marks)

**Question:** A rectangle has area 96 m². Its width is 4 m less than its length. Find whole-number dimensions and check the area.
**Answer:** Length 12 m; width 8 m
**Scene:** {'length': 12, 'width': 8, 'area': 96, 'units': 'm'}
**Whiteboard sequence:** A = l(l − 4) → 96 = l(l − 4) → 12 × 8 = 96 → l = 12 m, w = 8 m
**Teaching actions:** The width is four less than the length. → Find positive whole-number factors of ninety-six differing by four. → Twelve and eight satisfy both the area and side difference.
**Verification:** 12 × 8 = 96 m² and 12 − 8 = 4 m.
**Misconception:** Choosing factor pairs without checking the difference.
**Repair:** Arrange factor-pair rectangles and label both dimensions.

## Practice questions

1. Rectangle 6 cm by 3 cm: area in cm²?
   - Memo: 18. 6 × 3 = 18 cm².

2. Rectangle 8 m by 5 m: area in m²?
   - Memo: 40. 8 × 5 = 40 m².

3. Area 28 cm², width 4 cm: length in cm?
   - Memo: 7. 28 ÷ 4 = 7 cm.

4. Area 63 m², length 9 m: width in m?
   - Memo: 7. 63 ÷ 9 = 7 m.

5. Area 45 cm², width 5 cm: length in cm?
   - Memo: 9. 45 ÷ 5 = 9 cm.

6. Rectangle 7 cm by 4 cm: perimeter in cm?
   - Memo: 22. 2(7 + 4) = 22 cm.

7. Area 72 m², width 8 m: length in m?
   - Memo: 9. 72 ÷ 8 = 9 m.

8. Area 9 m², width 2.25 m: length in m?
   - Memo: 4. 9 ÷ 2.25 = 4 m.

9. Rectangle length (x+3) cm and width 2 cm: area at x=5, in cm²?
   - Memo: 16. 2(5 + 3) = 16 cm².

10. Area 48 m², width 6 m, tiles R50/m²: total tile cost in rand?
   - Memo: 2400. 48 × 50 = R2400.

11. Perimeter 34 cm, length 10 cm: width in cm?
   - Memo: 7. 34 ÷ 2 − 10 = 7 cm.

12. Area 50 cm², length 10 cm: perimeter in cm?
   - Memo: 30. Width = 5 cm, so P = 2(10 + 5) = 30 cm.

13. Area 98 cm², length twice width: positive width in cm?
   - Memo: 7. 2w² = 98, hence w² = 49 and w = 7 cm.

14. If x=4, length (x+6) cm and width x cm: area in cm²?
   - Memo: 40. (4 + 6) × 4 = 40 cm².

15. Area 80 m², width 2 m less than length: whole-number length in m?
   - Memo: 10. 10 × (10 − 2) = 80 m².
