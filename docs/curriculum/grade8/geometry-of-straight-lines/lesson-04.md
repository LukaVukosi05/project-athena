# Lesson 4: Unknowns in multi-step parallel-line diagrams

Objective: transfer an angle using the marked parallel lines, determine an adjacent or vertically opposite target, solve its linear expression and substitute into the original angle claim. Each of fifteen examples has independently calculated values. First locate the angle pair, then reveal its numeric intermediate, derive a target angle, form an equation, subtract the constant, divide by the coefficient and verify. Keep the numeric angle distinct from x. Later cases use co-interior and vertical relationships.

Independent practice changes both the upper angle and the algebraic expression. Five mastery items diagnose relationship selection, supplementary arithmetic and equation solving; three new variants reassess after targeted repair.

## Authored question bank

Each whiteboard example shows the problem, marked parallel lines and a visible first angle. The intermediate angle is concealed until its relationship is explained. The second angle and final answer appear only after their own mathematical steps. Previous results persist in the written solution.

### Worked example 1: g8-lines-04-w01
Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. The upper marked angle is 151°. The marked pair is corresponding. An angle beside the lower marked angle is labelled (2x + 9)°. Find x and justify both angle steps.

Diagram: corresponding_linear; given 151°, first intermediate sector 151°, target sector 29°; initial target label (2x + 9)°.
Whiteboard sequence:

1. Locate the two marked parallel lines and transversal; read the given 151° angle.
2. Identify the corresponding angle pair before calculating.
3. Corresponding angles are equal, so the lower marked angle is 151°.
4. Adjacent angles on a straight line add to 180°, so the target angle is 180° − 151° = 29°.
5. The marked target is 2x + 9 degrees, so 2x + 9 = 29.
6. Subtract 9 from both sides: 2x = 20.
7. Divide by 2: x = 10.

Answer: 10. Check: 2(10) + 9 = 29°; the original parallel-line pair checks against 151°.
Misconception: Use the named angle theorem, then the second relationship; do not measure the picture.
