# Grade 8 Integers — Lesson 3: Multiplying and dividing signed integers

## Learning aim
Reason about signs using mathematical structure, then check each result in the original expression.

## Worked whiteboard examples

### g8-int-l03-w01: Four groups of negative three
Problem: Calculate 4 × (−3) and explain the sign.
Visual family: groups. The initial board shows the complete expression but conceals the result.
Whiteboard steps:
1. Identify the operands and required operation in 4 × (−3).
2. Draw four equal groups with three negative counters in each group.
3. Count the twelve negative counters: 4 × (−3) = −12.
4. Check the inverse: −12 ÷ 4 = −3.
5. Conclude that 4 × (−3) = -12; check the preceding operations against the question.
Answer: -12.
Memo: 4 × (−3) = -12. Check the inverse: −12 ÷ 4 = −3.
Misconception: A sign pattern must be justified with grouping or inverse multiplication.

### g8-int-l03-w02: A negative number doubled
Problem: Calculate −5 × 2 and explain the sign.
Visual family: pattern. The initial board shows the complete expression but conceals the result.
Whiteboard steps:
1. Identify the operands and required operation in −5 × 2.
2. Two groups of −5 give −5 + (−5).
3. Add the groups to obtain −10; a negative times a positive is negative.
4. Check −10 ÷ 2 = −5.
5. Conclude that −5 × 2 = -10; check the preceding operations against the question.
Answer: -10.
Memo: −5 × 2 = -10. Check −10 ÷ 2 = −5.
Misconception: A sign pattern must be justified with grouping or inverse multiplication.

### g8-int-l03-w03: Extend a multiplication pattern
Problem: Calculate −4 × (−3) and explain the sign.
Visual family: sign_pattern. The initial board shows the complete expression but conceals the result.
Whiteboard steps:
1. Identify the operands and required operation in −4 × (−3).
2. Write −4 × 2 = −8, then −4 × 1 = −4, then −4 × 0 = 0.
3. Decreasing the second factor by one raises the product by four: −4 × (−1) = 4, then −4 × (−3) = 12.
4. Check 12 ÷ (−4) = −3.
5. Conclude that −4 × (−3) = 12; check the preceding operations against the question.
Answer: 12.
Memo: −4 × (−3) = 12. Check 12 ÷ (−4) = −3.
Misconception: A sign pattern must be justified with grouping or inverse multiplication.

### g8-int-l03-w04: Division as a missing factor
Problem: Calculate 18 ÷ (−3) and explain the sign.
Visual family: inverse. The initial board shows the complete expression but conceals the result.
Whiteboard steps:
1. Identify the operands and required operation in 18 ÷ (−3).
2. Ask which integer multiplied by −3 gives positive 18.
3. The magnitude is 18 ÷ 3 = 6 and the factors must have opposite signs, so the quotient is −6.
4. Check (−6) × (−3) = 18.
5. Conclude that 18 ÷ (−3) = -6; check the preceding operations against the question.
Answer: -6.
Memo: 18 ÷ (−3) = -6. Check (−6) × (−3) = 18.
Misconception: A sign pattern must be justified with grouping or inverse multiplication.

### g8-int-l03-w12: Keep an intermediate product
Problem: Calculate −6 × (−4) × (−2) and explain the sign.
Visual family: factor_chain. The initial board shows the complete expression but conceals the result.
Whiteboard steps:

3. Now multiply 24 by −2 to obtain −48; preserve the first result.
4. Check the factor signs: three negatives give a negative product.
5. Conclude that −6 × (−4) × (−2) = -48; check the preceding operations against the question.
Answer: -48.
Memo: −6 × (−4) × (−2) = -48. Check the factor signs: three negatives give a negative product.

### g8-int-l03-w13: Divide, then multiply
Problem: Calculate (−72 ÷ 8) × (−3) and explain the sign.
Visual family: factor_chain. The initial board shows the complete expression but conceals the result.
Whiteboard steps:
1. Identify the operands and required operation in (−72 ÷ 8) × (−3).
2. Evaluate the bracket first: −72 ÷ 8 = −9.
3. Multiply −9 by −3 to get positive 27.
4. Check 27 ÷ (−3) = −9, the bracket value.
5. Conclude that (−72 ÷ 8) × (−3) = 27; check the preceding operations against the question.
Answer: 27.
Memo: (−72 ÷ 8) × (−3) = 27. Check 27 ÷ (−3) = −9, the bracket value.
Misconception: A sign pattern must be justified with grouping or inverse multiplication.

### g8-int-l03-w14: Division associates left to right
Problem: Calculate −96 ÷ (−4) ÷ 3 and explain the sign.
Visual family: factor_chain. The initial board shows the complete expression but conceals the result.
Whiteboard steps:
1. Identify the operands and required operation in −96 ÷ (−4) ÷ 3.
2. Start from the left: −96 ÷ (−4) = 24.
3. Then 24 ÷ 3 = 8; do not combine the last two divisors first.
4. Check 8 × 3 × (−4) = −96.
5. Conclude that −96 ÷ (−4) ÷ 3 = 8; check the preceding operations against the question.
Answer: 8.
Memo: −96 ÷ (−4) ÷ 3 = 8. Check 8 × 3 × (−4) = −96.
Misconception: A sign pattern must be justified with grouping or inverse multiplication.

## Teaching and review
A wrong sign is diagnosed at the first mismatched group, inverse, exponent scope or calculation stage. Rebuild that stage, retain the previous operations, and try a parallel problem.
