# Grade 8 Pythagoras — Lesson 1: Squares on the sides of a right triangle

## Teaching contract
Show the problem and the question-specific triangle first. No final answer appears until the final result.
Reveal one operation at a time; retain every completed equation in the worked-solution history.
For a right triangle, the hypotenuse is opposite the right angle and must be longest. For the converse, show the given triangle without a right-angle mark until equality is established.
Squared lengths are areas; lengths use the positive square root. Preserve exact surds. A different triangle is used for practice and reassessment.

## Fifteen worked examples

### g8-pyth-01-w01
Question: A right-angled triangle has perpendicular sides of 3 cm and 4 cm. Find the areas of the squares on the two shorter sides and the square on the longest side.
Visual: family=square_areas; side a=3, side b=4, side c=None; context=triangle.
1. The two given sides meet at the marked right angle: 3 cm and 4 cm.
2. The first square has area 3 × 3 = 9 cm².
3. The second square has area 4 × 4 = 16 cm².
4. Add the two areas: 9 + 16 = 25 cm².
5. The square on the hypotenuse has area 25 cm².
Final answer: 9 cm², 16 cm²; hypotenuse square 25 cm²
Check: 9 + 16 = 25 cm²; areas, not side lengths, are added.
First error to diagnose: A side length is in cm; its square area is in cm². Do not add the side lengths.
