## g9-data-l07-w01: A transport survey counts walk 8, bus 12, car 16. Find the total and the exact pie-sector angle for walking.

Visual: {"family": "data", "values": {"lesson": 7, "model": "pie", "counts": [8, 12, 16], "labels": ["walk", "bus", "car"], "total": 36, "walk_angle_num": 80, "walk_angle_den": 1}}

1. Put walk 8, bus 12, car 16 in a frequency table.

2. Total responses = 8 + 12 + 16 = 36.

3. Walking is 8/36 of a whole circle.

4. Walk sector = (8/36) × 360° = 80°.

5. Check: each sector uses its count divided by 36; all three sector angles must total 360°.

**Answer:** total = 36; walking angle = 80°. **Misconception:** Keep the source, count, denominator and axis scale tied to the original dataset.

## g9-data-l07-w02: Five paired (study hours, score) readings are (1, 6), (2, 8), (3, 10), (4, 12), (5, 14). Describe the association and predict the score at 6 hours using the pattern.

Visual: {"family": "data", "values": {"lesson": 7, "model": "scatter", "points": [[1, 6], [2, 8], [3, 10], [4, 12], [5, 14]], "prediction": 16}}

1. Label study hours on the horizontal axis and score on the vertical axis.

2. Plot each given pair: (1, 6), (2, 8), (3, 10), (4, 12), (5, 14).

3. Every increase of one hour pairs with a score increase of 2.

4. The points show a positive association; the line rule for these data is score = 2h + 4.

5. Predict at 6 hours: 2(6) + 4 = 16; this is an extrapolation beyond the measured hours.

**Answer:** positive association; predicted score at 6 h = 16. **Misconception:** Keep the source, count, denominator and axis scale tied to the original dataset.
