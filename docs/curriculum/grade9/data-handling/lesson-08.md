## g9-data-l08-w01: A chart shows 37 and 43 responses but begins its bar axis at 32. State the true difference and percentage increase (two decimals), and describe why the chart misleads.

Visual: {"family": "data", "values": {"lesson": 8, "model": "misleading_bar", "first": 37, "second": 43, "truncated_baseline": 32, "difference": 6, "percent": "16.22"}}

1. Read actual labels 37 and 43, not just the visible bar lengths.

2. The axis starts at 32, not zero, so the visible bars exaggerate the relative change.

3. Actual difference = 43 − 37 = 6 responses.

4. Percentage increase = 6 ÷ 37 × 100 ≈ 16.22%.

5. Report: increase is 6 responses (16.22%); the truncated axis exaggerates its visual size.

**Answer:** difference = 6; increase ≈ 16.22%; truncated axis exaggerates change. **Misconception:** Keep the source, count, denominator and axis scale tied to the original dataset.
