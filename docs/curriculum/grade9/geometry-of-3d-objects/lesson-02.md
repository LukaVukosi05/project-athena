# Grade 9 Topic 14 · Lesson 2: Spheres and cylinders: curved and flat surfaces

Authority: [DBE 2026 Grade 9 Mathematics ATP](https://www.education.gov.za/Portals/0/Documents/Recovery%20plan%20page/2026%20ATPs/2026_ATP_Mathematics_Grade%209.pdf), Term 4. Original Athena questions.

Status: teacher and browser review required. Each operation updates the net/solid and a cumulative written solution.

## g9-solid-l02-w01: For a ball modelled as a sphere, count its flat faces, curved surfaces, edges and vertices. Explain how it can roll.

Visual: {"family": "solids", "values": {"lesson": 2, "model": "sphere", "context": "a ball"}}

1. Identify one continuous curved surface.

2. Check for flat faces: there are none.

3. Trace boundaries: there are no edges or vertices.

4. A sphere rolls in every direction because its whole surface curves.

5. Check: 0 flat faces, 1 curved surface, 0 edges, 0 vertices.

**Answer:** sphere: 0 flat faces, 1 curved surface, 0 edges, 0 vertices. **Misconception:** Count actual face pieces and their joining edges; keep curved surfaces distinct from flat faces.

## g9-solid-l02-w02: For a tin modelled as a closed cylinder, count its flat circular faces, curved surfaces, curved edges and vertices.

Visual: {"family": "solids", "values": {"lesson": 2, "model": "cylinder", "context": "a tin"}}

1. Identify the top circular base.

2. Identify the matching bottom circular base.

3. Trace the lateral curved surface and its two circular boundary edges.

4. The two round boundaries are curved edges, not vertices.

5. Check: 2 flat circular faces, 1 curved surface, 2 curved edges, 0 vertices.

**Answer:** cylinder: 2 flat circular faces, 1 curved surface, 2 curved edges, 0 vertices. **Misconception:** Count actual face pieces and their joining edges; keep curved surfaces distinct from flat faces.

## g9-solid-l02-w03: Compare a closed cylindrical a can with a spherical model: which has circular flat faces, and which has no edges at all?

Visual: {"family": "solids", "values": {"lesson": 2, "model": "compare", "context": "a can"}}

1. Place the sphere and closed cylinder side by side.

2. Mark the cylinder’s two flat circular bases.

3. Trace its two curved boundary edges.

4. The sphere has one uninterrupted curved surface with no edges.

5. Check: cylinder has flat circles; sphere has no edges.

**Answer:** cylinder: circular flat faces; sphere: no edges. **Misconception:** Count actual face pieces and their joining edges; keep curved surfaces distinct from flat faces.
