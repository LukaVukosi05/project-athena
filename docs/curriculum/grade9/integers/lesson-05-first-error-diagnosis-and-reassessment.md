## Worked examples

### g9-int-l05-w01 · foundation
Question: A learner wrote 7 − (-4) = 3. Find the first incorrect operation and give the corrected result.
Givens: 7 − (-4) = 3
Visual: {"family": "diagnosis", "values": [7, -4], "operator": "subtraction", "path": [3, 11], "learner_claim": "7 − (-4) = 3"}
1. Read the learner claim: 7 − (-4) = 3.
2. The first error is treating subtraction of -4 as addition of -4; add the opposite 4.
3. Calculate 7 + 4 = 11; compare with the claim.
4. Check: The corrected calculation evaluates to 11, while the learner claim gives 3.
Answer: 11
Misconception: first-wrong-subtraction

### g9-int-l05-p01
Question: A learner wrote 9 − (-5) = 4. Find the first incorrect operation and give the corrected result.
A. 14
B. 4
C. 9
D. -14
Key: A
Memo: 14. Read the learner claim: 9 − (-5) = 4. The first error is treating subtraction of -5 as addition of -5; add the opposite 5. Calculate 9 + 5 = 14; compare with the claim. Check: The corrected calculation evaluates to 14, while the learner claim gives 4.
Distractor 4: Incorrect: Keeps the original incorrect result.
Distractor 9: Incorrect: Stops at the unchanged first value.
Distractor -14: Incorrect: Reverses the sign of the repaired result.
