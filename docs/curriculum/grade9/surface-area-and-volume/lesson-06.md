## g9-sv-l06-w01: An open-top rectangular box has length 6 cm, width 4 cm, height 5 cm. A learner includes a top face. Find the first error and the required material area.

Visual: {"family": "surface_volume", "values": {"lesson": 6, "model": "applied_error", "kind": "open_box", "length": 6, "width": 4, "height": 5, "correct_value": 124}}

1. Identify the base 6 cm × 4 cm; the top is open.

2. A closed prism has two bases, but this net has only one.

3. First error: the learner added a top face that does not exist.

4. Material area = 24 + 2(30) + 2(20) = 124 cm².

5. Check one base and four sides, no top; units are cm².

**Answer:** first error: included a top; material area = 124 cm². **Misconception:** Distinguish length, square and cubic units; count each face once, then verify the given radius, height and conversion.

## g9-sv-l06-w02: A triangular prism has a right-triangle end with legs 9 cm and 12 cm, and length 6 cm. A learner omits ½. Find the first error and correct volume.

## g9-sv-l06-w03: A closed cylinder has diameter 10 cm and height 7 cm. A learner squares the diameter as the radius. Find the first error and correct volume in terms of π.

Visual: {"family": "surface_volume", "values": {"lesson": 6, "radius": 5, "diameter": 10, "height": 7, "model": "applied_error", "kind": "cylinder", "length": 0, "width": 0, "correct_value": 175}}

1. The given 10 cm is a diameter.

2. Radius = diameter ÷ 2 = 5 cm.

3. First error: squaring 10 instead of the radius 5.

4. Volume = π × 5² × 7 = 175π cm³.

5. Check: using the diameter would produce four times the correct volume.

**Answer:** first error: used diameter; volume = 175π cm³. **Misconception:** Distinguish length, square and cubic units; count each face once, then verify the given radius, height and conversion.

## g9-sv-l06-w04: A rectangular prism has dimensions 9 cm × 3 cm × 8 cm. A learner doubles its volume when all dimensions double. Find the first error and new volume.

Visual: {"family": "surface_volume", "values": {"lesson": 6, "model": "applied_error", "kind": "double_all", "length": 9, "width": 3, "height": 8, "correct_value": 1728}}

1. Original volume = 9 × 3 × 8 = 216 cm³.

2. New dimensions are 18, 6, 16 cm.

3. First error: doubling all three dimensions multiplies volume by 8, not 2.

4. New volume = 18 × 6 × 16 = 1728 cm³.

5. Check: 1728/216 = 8.

**Answer:** first error: used factor 2; new volume = 1728 cm³. **Misconception:** Distinguish length, square and cubic units; count each face once, then verify the given radius, height and conversion.

## g9-sv-l06-w05: A rectangular container measures 10 cm × 4 cm × 4 cm inside. A learner divides cm³ by 100 to obtain litres. Find the first error and correct capacity.

Correct: first error: divided by 100; capacity = 28/25 L. Volume = 70 × 4 × 4 = 1120 cm³. The learner divided by 100, which converts square units rather than cubic capacity. First error: 1 L = 1 000 cm³, so divide by 1000. Correct capacity = 1120/1000 = 28/25 L. Check: 28/25 × 1000 = 1120 cm³.
