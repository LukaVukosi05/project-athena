## Worked examples

### g9-whole-l04-w01 · foundation
Question: 3 identical notebooks cost R45. At the same price per item, what do 7 cost?
Givens: 3 items; R45; 7 items
Visual: direct · {"family": "direct", "table": [[3, 45], [7, 105]], "units": ["items", "R"]}
1. Test the relationship: more notebooks means more cost at a fixed unit price.
2. One item costs R45÷3 = R15.
3. Multiply the unit cost by 7: R15×7 = R105.
4. Check: 105÷7=15 and 45÷3=15 rand per item.
Answer: R105
Misconception: direct-scale

### g9-whole-l04-w06 · standard
Question: 4 painters finish an equal-size job in 12 days. At the same individual rate, how many days will 6 painters need?
Givens: 4 painters; 12 days; 6 painters
Visual: inverse · {"family": "inverse", "table": [[4, 12], [6, 8]], "units": ["workers", "days"]}
1. Test the relationship: more painters need fewer days for the same job.
2. The fixed job needs 4×12=48 worker-days.
3. Divide by 6 workers: 48÷6=8 days.
4. Check: 6×8=4×12=48 worker-days.
Answer: 8 days
Misconception: direct-instead-of-inverse

### g9-whole-l04-w11 · exam

### g9-whole-l04-p01
Question: 3 identical notebooks cost R60. At the same price per item, what do 7 cost?
A. R140
B. R420
C. R67
D. R20
Key: A
Memo: R140. Test the relationship: more notebooks means more cost at a fixed unit price. One item costs R60÷3 = R20. Multiply the unit cost by 7: R20×7 = R140. Check: 140÷7=20 and 60÷3=20 rand per item.
Distractor R420: Incorrect: Multiplies total price by item count.
Distractor R67: Incorrect: Adds a count to a currency amount.
Distractor R20: Incorrect: Stops at one unit instead of the requested count.
